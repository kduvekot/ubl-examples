How UBL helps

A supplier to a retailer shares more than orders: sales per store, stock policies and agreed exception thresholds. UBL's retail collaboration documents carry them, and a Work Report documents installation work for the customer to approve.

In this world it sends 19 and receives 17 documents, and is named in 4 documents between other parties. They are listed per flow below.

Wood: slats for the benches and timber for furniture (Bayvarra → Urvania, and at home)

Marrowdale Sawmill in Upland State saws Valdara's bench slats to Valdara's drawing, 1800 × 95 × 38 mm, kiln dried, and sells them by the bundle of 50. Valdara asks for a quotation, which the sawmill gives under Valdara's purchasing terms, for oak with ash as a priced alternative, and orders 96 bundles delivered at its factory (DAP), so the sawmill books the truck across the border: 4,800 slats on 24 heat-treated pallets, about 22.2 t, a quarter's supply for 960 benches. This delivery is oak; from May the sawmill saws ash, as its felling in the managed forest moves to an ash stand. The furniture maker buys timber at home; selling for manufacture, against the furniture maker's exemption certificate, the sawmill charges no sales tax and says why (decision D43).

Furniture for the department store and for public offices (Bayvarra)

The furniture maker makes the department store's own-label dining furniture, the Elderwick Marrow bench and chair, each in oak and in ash, and supplies it piece by piece: each wood is an item of its own, because the shopper chooses it. The line is for the reopening of the store's home and garden department on Monday 11 May: the stock is delivered in March and held for it, and the first sale is on the day of the reopening. They agree on the item data and the stock policy per piece, the store reports what it sells, and exceptions against agreed thresholds are flagged automatically. The new line is taken on sale-or-return terms for a trial of its first four weeks of sales, and what is above the target at the end of the trial goes back in the store's van, at the maker's cost: the maker credits it in full and the store invoices the transport. The store asked for seat pads as an option; the maker quoted one, and the store sells pads from its own textile range instead. For the public works department the furniture maker also installs, and documents the work for approval.
