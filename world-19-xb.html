#19 Customs (XB)

Country B (XB) · Government

Import customs at the land border crossing and at the seaport.

How UBL helps

Import customs checks goods arriving over the land border and through the seaport. UBL import and transit declarations carry the same consignment and goods data as the export declaration on the other side, which makes it possible to compare them.

In this world it sends 0 and receives 2 documents, and is named in 0 documents between other parties. They are listed per flow below.

Finished goods by truck across the land border (Country A → Country B)

The distributor buys free carrier (FCA) at the manufacturer's warehouse. Because the buyer arranges the transport, the distributor books the road carrier itself and tells the seller in the order which truck company will pick the goods up and where. The seller's 3PL hands the goods over; the seller clears them for export, the distributor for import.

Receives

  • Import Customs Declaration from #6 Distributor: The distributor, as importer, declares the goods in Country B and claims the preferential duty rate with the certificate of origin.

    In it as CustomsParty.

    Also mentions: #5 Manufacturer as ConsignorParty.

Overseas by container ship, cost insurance and freight (Country B → island Country C)

The commercial buyer on the island buys cost, insurance and freight (CIF) to the port of discharge. The distributor therefore pays the sea freight and the cargo insurance; it hands both to its freight forwarder. The buyer takes over at the port of discharge: its customs broker clears the goods and its 3PL receives them.

Receives

  • Export Customs Declaration from #10 Freight Forwarder: The forwarder, as customs agent, declares the export for the distributor at the seaport.

    In it as CustomsDeclaration/CustomsParty.

    Also mentions: #6 Distributor as ExporterParty; #1 Commercial Buyer as CustomsDeclaration/ConsigneeParty.
