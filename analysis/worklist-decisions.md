### D9 · In a debit note issued by the buyer, is the buyer the accounting supplier?
- Why it matters: Cause C66 Debit notes with accounting supplier and customer reversed; 5 findings (5 marked "yes").
- What the documents say now: All three debit notes (furniture/14, sea/28, tender/34) carry the issuer as accounting customer and the debited party as accounting supplier; their numbers and language tags name the real issuer. - Options:
  - a) The issuer (creditor) is the accounting supplier: swap the roles in documents.json (3 documents, plus tender/35 and 36 then stand).

- Recommendation: a): it matches UBL's definitions and all reviews; one data change.
- Related: none
- **Decided (owner, 2026-11-16): neither a) nor b) as written. In this world a debit note is the supplier's addition to an invoice it issued, the counterpart of the credit note.** An invoice was issued; nothing is to be credited, but more should have been charged; the supplier sends a debit note for that part, with a reference to the invoice and the reason. This is UBL's meaning: the definitions mirror each other (credit note: "credits due to the Debtor from the Creditor"; debit note: "debits due to the Creditor from the Debtor"); the specification names the Supplier Accounting Party as submitter and the Customer Accounting Party as receiver, and says "The supplier may then issue a Credit Note, a Debit Note, or another Invoice as required" and "A Credit Note or Debit Note may also be issued in the case of retrospective price change" (UBL 2.5, 2.3.7.1.3 and 3.2.19); the structure has `BillingReference` to the invoice, `DiscrepancyResponse` ("A reason for the debit") and `DebitedQuantity`; the OASIS sample debit note is a supplier's note against its invoice INV000123.
- **What follows:** the three debit notes of the world are all a buyer charging its supplier (furniture/14: the store bills the maker for return transport; sea/28: the importer passes on a customs inspection cost; tender/34: the public buyer claims a contract penalty). None adds to an invoice the issuer had sent, so none is a debit note in this sense. Each is re-homed with its flow: furniture (D32), sea (D30), tender (D28). A service the buyer performed is an invoice from the buyer; a cost passed on is an invoice, or a credit note from the supplier; a penalty is a credit note from the supplier or a deduction shown in the remittance advice. The earlier recommendation (swap the roles so that the issuing buyer is the accounting supplier) is withdrawn.
- **Open: where the world has a real debit note.** Two candidates were looked at and set aside by the owner: the ore train's bonus (a bonus earned is known before the carrier invoices and belongs on the invoice) and the extra box of side frames in the road flow (an over-delivery from a picking mistake is a poor reason for a document type). - **Not forced (owner, 2026-11-16).** The place of the world's real debit note stays open until a flow yields a natural case. First candidate: demurrage in the rail or sea flow. The rail plans say "Free time five days at the siding, then demurrage per container day": the freight invoice is right when it goes out, and whether demurrage is due is only known when the container is released, days later. In the sea flow nothing covers the container between its release on 19 May and the receipt on 21 May (timelines#F7), which demurrage could explain. Second candidate: waiting time ("Waiting time 40 per hour beyond two hours" in the ore, plastics and rail plans). If no flow yields a case, the world has no debit note; under the no-fillers rule that is acceptable, and the element coverage of the debit note then drops.
- **Noted for the story:** Valdara invoices on despatch (road: invoice on 24 March, the day the goods cross the border; receipt advice on 25 March). UBL names this as the normal case ("The Invoice is normally issued on the basis of one despatch event triggering one Invoice") beside "Invoice after return of Receipt Advice". The extra box of side frames in the road flow is a loose end (reported over on receipt, then neither billed nor returned); it is settled with D35.

### Identification, the GS1 way and the Codana way (D5)

**Two systems, chosen by who a company trades with.**
- **GS1** is the system of Bayvarra's retail and distribution chain: Lumeway Distribution, Brightmarket Stores and Brackenmere Furniture. Their company prefixes start with 952, which GS1 reserves for examples. **GS1 Bayvarra** licenses the prefixes: a not-for-profit association owned by its member companies, begun around 1981 as an article numbering association when Brightmarket and other retailers wanted bar codes at the till, and renamed in 2005 when the worldwide organisation took the name GS1 (the real history: the European association started in 1977, the name GS1 dates from 2005). It gives a company a seven-digit company prefix; the company builds its own GTINs, GLNs, SSCCs and GRAIs from it. GS1 Bayvarra also serves Lisavu, which has no organisation of its own. It is part of the story, not a party in the documents, until a document has a reason to name it.
- **Codana**, the Codana Identification Agency, is Urvania's public numbering body, founded in 1999 when the country opened its trade. It is an issuing agency under ISO/IEC 15459 with the agency code `KXU`, and keeps the organisation register behind the ISO/IEC 6523 code `0990`. It registers any company that asks, foreign ones too: Rivermouth Packaging is Bayvarran, but its customer is Valdara, so it uses its customer's system.
- **Own numbers only**: the island's cotton farm and ginnery, the sawmill, the engineering company and the utilities.

**Codana and GS1 Bayvarra side by side.**

| | Codana | GS1 Bayvarra |
|---|---|---|
| Country | Urvania | Bayvarra (also serves Lisavu) |
| What it is | A public body, set up by the state | An association of companies |
| Founded | 1999, when Urvania opened its trade | Around 1981, by retailers and distributors |
| Standard | ISO/IEC 15459 and ISO/IEC 6523 | GS1 |
| Gives a company | A five-digit company number | A seven-digit company prefix |

**Every register issues its own numbers (owner, 2026-11-15).** A company's Codana number or GS1 company prefix is not its business register number and not its tax number: each is issued by a different body, in that body's own series, and none is derived from another. No number may occur in a similar form in two numbering schemes: each scheme has its own shape, so that a value shows which scheme it belongs to, and no value of one scheme repeats the digits of a value of another. The build checks this.

What the documents do today, and what becomes of it (owner, 2026-11-15):
- **Codana number:** today the business register number cut to five digits (`0990:10427` from `XU-R-104274`; Rivermouth's `0990:20274` from `20412740`). **Changes:** Codana issues its numbers in order of registration since 1999, unrelated to the business register; a large early member such as Valdara has a low one.
- **Telephone numbers:** today they end in the party's register number (`+999 1 041 518 4274`). **Changes:** no relation to any identifier.
- **Electronic address (endpoint):** the country and the register number (`XU:XU-R-104274`). **Stays, as a stated rule:** the network addresses a company by a number it already has, as Peppol does; an electronic address is always an existing identifier under a scheme code and never a new number.
- **Customs trader number:** repeats the register number in all three countries (`XU-TR-104274` from `XU-R-104274`, `XB-TR-410274` from `20410274`, `XL-TR-041859` from `IC41859`). **Stays, as a stated rule** (settled by D18): customs registers a trader under its business register number.
- **Build check:** no identifier of a party repeats the digits of another of its identifiers, apart from the stated reuse in the electronic address (and the customs trader number, if that is kept); each scheme keeps its own shape.

The shapes in use, per country: Urvania: register `XU-R-` and six digits, tax `XU` and nine digits, customs trader `XU-TR-` and six digits. Bayvarra: register eight digits starting `2041`, tax the state's two letters and seven digits (`CO-1238220`), customs trader `XB-TR-` and six digits. Lisavu: register `IC` and five digits, customs trader `XL-TR-` and six digits, no tax number. Codana: five digits, always written with `0990:` or `KXU`. GS1 Bayvarra: seven digits starting `952`.

**One company number, two uses.** A company registers once with Codana and gets a five-digit number. That number is its party identifier (`0990:10427` for Valdara Industries) and the root of everything it labels (`KXU10427…`). This is how the real non-GS1 systems work: a D&B DUNS number is ISO/IEC 6523 code 0060 and agency code `UN`; an Odette number is 0177 and `OD`; GS1 itself is 0088 (the GLN) and the agency codes `0` to `9`.

**Codana's rule book.**
- Company prefix: `KXU` followed by the five-digit company number (8 characters).
- After the prefix comes the company's own reference, in upper-case letters and digits only, without hyphens or spaces.
- No identifier is longer than 20 characters, which ISO/IEC 15459 recommends for a one-line bar code (the standard allows 50 for items, serials and lots, and 35 for transport units).
- There is no check character, as in ISO/IEC 15459 itself. The build checks instead that the prefix of every identifier is the company number of the party that issued it.
- Codana does not hand out item numbers. It gives a company its number; the company numbers its own items, lots, pallets and places.

**What each thing looks like.** The examples use the company numbers the documents have today (`10427` for Valdara, `10441` for Castrano), which are cut from the business register number; under the rule that every register issues its own numbers, Codana gives new ones in order of registration (open point O22), and the examples change with them.

| Thing | The company's own number | Globally unique form (Codana) | ISO/IEC 15459 part, data identifier | GS1 side |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Item (the bench) | `VI-B180` | `KXU10427B180` | part 6, 25P | GTIN (Lumeway's `9520610000012`) |
| One serialised item (a casting) | `VI-SF-01230` | `KXU10427SF01230` | part 4, 25S | serialised GTIN |
| Production lot | `VI-LOT-260510` | `KXU10427LOT260510` | part 6, 25T | GTIN and lot |
| Transport unit (a pallet) | none | `KXU104411490000015` (Castrano Logistics) | part 1, J | SSCC |
| Returnable transport item | none | prefix, type and serial | part 5, 25B | GRAI |
| Site | none | prefix and site code | 25L | GLN |
| Company | register number | `0990:10427` | ISO/IEC 6523 (18V in a bar code) | GLN |

The data identifier is the prefix a bar code uses to say what follows; in a UBL document the element says that, so it is not part of the value. In a document the company's own number stays where it is (the seller's item identification, the serial, the lot); the globally unique form is the standard identification, given where a document crosses company borders and the receiver needs it.

**What the examples show with it.**
- The bench crosses from one system to the other: Valdara ships it as `KXU10427B180`; Lumeway gives it a GTIN for the shops and carries the maker's number alongside, as an importer may.
- A pallet needs no relabelling: GS1 is itself an ISO/IEC 15459 agency, so Lumeway's warehouse reads Castrano's licence plate and its own SSCCs alike.
- One company number serves as party identifier and as the root of the company's labels.

### Billing documents: invoice, credit note, debit note (D9)

- **An invoice** requests payment. A supplier may invoice on despatch, as Valdara Industries does, or after the customer's receipt advice.
- **A credit note** is the supplier's document for what the customer does not owe after all.
- **A debit note** is its counterpart: the supplier's addition to an invoice it issued, when more is due than was charged. It refers to that invoice and gives the reason. It is not a buyer's claim against its supplier.
- **When a buyer has a claim on its supplier** (a service the buyer performed, a cost it had to bear, a contract penalty), the world uses an invoice from the buyer, a credit note from the supplier, or a deduction shown in the remittance advice, as the flow's story requires.

### What a document says about an item (D11)

**An item's facts come in three layers, each known at a different moment.**

| Layer | Known when | Examples in the world | Stated first in |
|---|---|---|---|
| The item type and its variants | When the product is designed | Dimensions, materials, the standard colours (the grey bench is the variant `GRY`), the warranty, the footprint per bench, the commodity code | The catalogue, the product information |
| The lot or the single piece | When it is made, graded or tested | The lot `VI-LOT-260510` and its manufacture date, a casting's serial and melt number, the classing grade and moisture of a cotton lot | The document that accompanies the goods: the despatch advice, a certificate, a weight statement |
| The transaction | When it is ordered, shipped, measured or cleared | Which variant is chosen, anything made to order (a special colour), the quantity and price, the actual gross weight of a shipment, a seal number, a meter reading, the customs value | The transaction's own document: the order, the despatch advice, the invoice, the declaration |

**The rule.**
1. A fact is stated first in the document of the moment it becomes known.
2. A later document repeats a fact only if its own transaction uses it. Otherwise it identifies the item, the lot or the earlier document, and the reader finds the fact there.

The rule works in both directions: a catalogue with a lot number is as wrong as an invoice with the search keywords. Companies keep their item data in step through catalogues; every later document points to the item by its identifier. A reader who follows one bench through its documents sees it gain facts: a type in the catalogue, a variant in the order, a lot and serial numbers when it leaves the factory, a weight and a seal on the truck, a value at the border.

**Colour.** The colours an item comes in are type facts, listed in the catalogue as variants; an order names the variant it wants by its identifier and states nothing new about the item. Only a colour the catalogue does not offer is specified in the order, as something made to order.

**A starting profile per document type** (to be worked out at implementation):

| Document | What it carries about the item |
|---|---|
| Catalogue, item information, product passport | Everything about the type and its variants |
| Order, order response | Identification, name, the variant chosen, anything made to order |
| Despatch and receipt advice | Identification, name, lot or serial numbers, handling and dangerous-goods data |
| Invoice, credit note | Identification, name, and what tax needs |
| Customs declaration | Description, commodity code, origin, weights |
| Shopper's receipt | Name, brand, and the warranty, as the shopper's proof |

### What a document says about a party (open point O86, decided 2026-11-16)

- **Decided:** each party is the authority on its own data, and every document stands on its own.
  1. A document carries what its receiver needs to know who it is dealing with, to the depth that suits the kind of document. Nothing is left out because an earlier document said it: no party may rely on the other having kept an older document.
  2. The sender describes itself in full for that kind of document. It describes the other party with what it knows and needs, which may be little.
  3. The answer completes the picture: in the response the other party describes itself in full, and may add to or correct what the first document said about it. Pairs where this happens: order and order response; request for quotation and quotation; transport execution plan request and transport execution plan; forwarding instructions and waybill; call for tenders and tender; qualification request and its answer; catalogue request and catalogue; despatch advice and receipt advice.
  4. A third party (a carrier in a despatch advice, a bank, an originator) gets what the receiver needs to recognise or reach it: identification, name, and a contact or address where the document's job needs one.
- **Depth by kind of document:** address, contact and electronic address in trade documents; the legal entity in contracts, tenders, qualifications and invoices; the tax registration in documents that carry tax (the customer's only where the tax rule needs it); the customs trader number in customs and cross-border transport documents; industry class, website, logo, shareholders and permits in documents whose job is to present the party (the provider of a catalogue, a tender qualification), and a permit where the document depends on it (the waste carrier); share capital on an issuer's own invoice where its law asks for it. Each of these has two answers: "about yourself" and "about the other".
- **`Language`:** a party states its own language, with its `LocaleCode` (D1), so that the answer comes in it. It states the other party's language only where it knows it.
- **Rule for the build:** what A says about B may be less than what B says about itself, and must never contradict it, except in the one correction case (O89).
- **What it means for the world:** master data is not copied from one record into every document. A buyer's order names the seller by number, name and the address it has on file; the order response carries the seller's own legal entity, its sales contact for this order and its language. The world gains the question "who knows what about whom, and from when".
- **Findings:** cause C01 (245) is resolved by this rule; it is the party-side twin of D11.
- **Coverage:** the owner wants to see what the rule does to coverage when it is applied (O88).

### Customs between the three countries (D16; D17 and D18 to follow)

- **Valuation is national.** The Accord is a free trade area, not a customs union; each member values imports its own way, within the WTO's rules. Urvania includes freight and insurance to its border, in the continental way, like its value added tax. Bayvarra values goods as they left the exporting country, without freight, in the settlers' tradition (as the United States, Canada and Australia do). Lisavu, an island that lives on import taxes, values everything landed.
- **A seller states its own country's tax.** An Urvanian seller's invoice shows Urvanian value added tax or the reason none is due; a Bayvarran seller's shows the state sales tax or an exemption; a Lisavan seller's shows no tax section (D15, D17).
- **Bayvarra taxes what foreign businesses earn from its public purse, at source.** A public body withholds 10 % of the price of a service from a business not established in Bayvarra and pays it to the state. It is a tax on income, not a sales tax, and it applies to public bodies only: a company such as Lumeway withholds nothing from a foreign carrier. The foreign supplier names a tax representative in Bayvarra, registered with the tax office (D17).
- **Urvania does not tax a service supplied outside Urvania:** to a customer established abroad, or on land abroad (D17).
- **Goods of Accord origin cross the borders free of duty, on proof of origin.** Goods from outside the Accord pay each country's own tariff. What is still due at the border differs: in Urvania, value added tax of 20 % on the value with freight and insurance to the border, which a business deducts again; in Bayvarra, nothing, because its sales tax is charged once at the final retail sale; in Lisavu, the import consumption tax of 10 % on the landed value (D18).
- **What Lisavu gave up in 1996.** When the Accord came into force the island stopped charging duty on its partners' goods and kept the import consumption tax, which falls on every import whatever its origin. It is the island's substitute for a sales tax and the reason it could afford to join. Imported goods cost more on the island than on the mainland: freight, and 10 % on top (D18).
- **Customs registers a trader under its business register number** (D18).
- **What is common:** the value starts from the price actually paid, and charges that are part of that price (a special finish, export packing) belong to it.

### Money: three currencies at fixed parities (open point O2)

- **The parities.** 1 lume = 100 ferra = 0.4 coral. They are fixed by the Accord's monetary protocol and reviewed every five years, against how the three economies have developed. This is an adjustable peg, as the Bretton Woods system was and the European exchange-rate mechanism with its realignments; the West African CFA franc held one parity for decades and was then devalued in one step, in 1994.
- **History.** The coral has been tied to the lume since the Lumeport Agreement of 1974: an island that sells its cotton in lume has every reason to tie its money to it. The ferra joined at a fixed parity when Urvania came into the Accord in 1996. The last review, on 1 January 2022, left the parities unchanged. The next is on 1 January 2027.
- **What it means for the documents.** Through 2026 every exchange rate in the examples is the parity; nothing moves. There is still a daily official rate, the noon fixing of the Lumeport Exchange, which does not change between reviews. The Accord's size classes give their turnover limits in all three currencies at the parities.
- **Currency risk is real across a review.** Anything agreed now and paid after 1 January 2027 may be paid at another rate. A forward exchange contract protects a long commitment across the review date. The world's case is the two-year framework contract of the tender flow: it is priced in lume, Valdara's costs are in ferra, so the risk is Valdara's and the forward is Valdara's, made after the award, for the releases due after the review (the direction is decided; how the documents carry it is open point O50). (Today the documents show a forward dated 2 January 2026, before the procedure began, read as the buyer's hedge, on the pricing rate of six documents, at exactly the parity.)
- **Not chosen:** a spot conversion contract at the parity (it would state nothing); floating rates (every amount in two currencies would need a rate per date).

Open threads: O50 (the forward contract in the tender flow), O51 (a trading partner outside the world, later), O52 (earlier reviews, for the encyclopaedia).

### Geography: where the world lies (D19)

- **The mainland** is a land of about 560 by 390 km in the middle of the South Atlantic, astride the Mid-Atlantic Ridge, far from every continent: Urvania in the west, inland; Bayvarra in the east, on the coast, with Lumeport on its east shore facing the open ocean. Each country is about the size of Portugal. (Current base: 28.1° to 31.9° South, 17.7° to 12.3° West; the high seas, outside every country's waters and claims.)
- **Lisavu** is an isolated island about 1,000 km further east, the size of Cyprus, a volcanic island on a seamount of the Walvis Ridge (current base: 33.0° South, 2.65° West). From the mainland the first sight of it is a wall of cliffs: its west coast faces the prevailing westerly wind and the ocean swell and has no landing. Ships round a cape to the lee side, where the port, the airport, the town of Selanu and the cotton fields lie, in the island's rain shadow. An island cut off like this has to work for its ties, which is the story of the Lumeport Agreement of 1974 and of the shipping line founded to serve it.
- **Distances.** One map square is 14 km. By road, Ferrano to Marrowdale is about 182 km and Marrowdale to Lumeport about 84 km; the ore train runs about 154 km. The sea crossing to Selanu takes about 42 hours, the freighter's flight about two hours. The island's clocks are an hour ahead of the mainland's (D27).
- **On the map of the site** the island stands in an inset, as distant territory does on real maps (Shetland on maps of Britain, Hawaii on maps of the United States).

### Geography: the map is a diagram (open point O82, decided 2026-11-16)

- **What was found:** the map is drawn like a board, every party on a tile of its own with room around it, and a town as a circle 8 to 10 squares across. At 14 km a square (D19) that puts Lumeport's port 140 km from Lumeport, Valdara's works 57 km from Ferrano's centre, the two customs posts of one border 42 km apart and a shop 31 km from its shopper.
- **Decided (owner):** the drawing is a diagram, as a metro map is; it is not to scale inside and around the towns. The geography comes from a place list:
  - each place has one position at 14 km a square;
  - each party belongs to a place and lies a few kilometres from its centre (a port on the town's waterfront, an airport 10 to 20 km out, customs posts facing each other);
  - distances between places come from a table (the figures of D19), not from counting tiles; coordinates in the documents come from the place and the party's offset in it.
- **Rule for the drawing:** the diagram may stretch distances and must be true in everything else: what lies on the coast is drawn on the coast, on the right side of a border, in the right order along a route, next to its real neighbours, and in its own town. Where the drawing breaks this rule it is redrawn (O92).
- **What stays from D19:** 14 km a square for the countries' sizes and the positions of places; the long distances and times; the island overseas in an inset.
- **What it means for the world:** towns become towns: Lumeport is a coastal city with its port in it. Nearly every party's coordinates change; stated distances inside a town become a few kilometres. The place list is new world data and the skeleton of the encyclopaedia (towns, districts, what stands where).

### The park bench Serra 180, as built and shipped (D20)

- **What it is:** two cast and galvanised steel side frames of 10 kg each, five hardwood slats of 1,800 × 95 × 38 mm at 4.5 kg each, and a set of 20 bolts: 43.5 kg. It is bolted to the ground with four anchors, which are a separate item.

### Work and safety: the handling limit (D20)

The three countries apply the limit of ISO 11228-1 for manual handling: 25 kg for one person in good conditions, and about two thirds of the combined capacity for two people lifting together. Goods are packed so that a parcel a person lifts stays within it; anything heavier is moved by machine, and the documents say so. It is the kind of rule a trade agreement's labour chapter shares between its members.

### The goods chain of the park bench (D21)

- **Valdara makes to order, in lots.** A lot has a date, a quantity, a coating batch; every side frame has a serial number that rises with the day it was cast, and every casting day has its own melt number.
- **Lumeway holds stock in Marrowdale.** It received 96 benches and 12 spare frames by road on 25 March and 192 benches by rail on 15 April, and sells from that stock in the order the goods arrived: 24 benches to Brightmarket on 29 April, 96 benches and the 12 frames to Velumi on the island on 13 May. At the end of May it still holds 168 benches.
- **An urgent order does not come from stock.** When Velumi asks for eight more frames on 11 May, Lumeway has none; Valdara casts them and they are passed through Lumeway's dispatch area to the aircraft, a cross-dock.
- **Valdara also sells direct:** 40 benches to the public works department on 30 March, from a lot of their own, and two benches travel to a fair under a carnet.

### Payment: whose terms apply (D22; D23 to follow)

- **Three layers.** First, what the parties agreed in a contract or a framework agreement. Where they agreed nothing, the seller's standard terms, which follow its country's practice: Urvania gives 2 % discount for payment within ten days of 30 days net; Bayvarra charges a surcharge per month late on 30 days net; the island gives 45 days. Where neither says anything about late payment, the statutory interest of the seller's country (in Urvania 8 % a year).
- **Across a border** the seller's country's rule applies, unless the contract chooses another.
- **Utility bills are due in 21 days,** in all three countries, by a rule of the Accord (D23). A standard terms code means one text and one period wherever it is used.
- **Examples in the world:** Valdara's framework agreement with Ferrano Rail Cargo sets 30 days net from the consignment note, so the carrier's invoices carry no discount and fall due from the consignment note. The public works contract sets 30 days after each accepted release, so Valdara's invoice falls due from the acceptance, not from its own date.

### Utilities: public services the Accord leaves national (D23)

- **Each country keeps its own.** The supply of electricity, gas, water and telephone to end customers is a national matter under the Accord: each member keeps its utility, public or under national licence, and no utility of one member supplies end customers in another. Ferrano Energy serves Urvania, the Coastal State Water Company serves Bayvarra's Coastal State, Selanu Telecom serves the island. There is no cross-border utility.
- **Wholesale energy does cross the border.** The Accord's energy protocol of 2007 opened the trade in gas and electricity between producers, networks and suppliers.
- **Networks under the protocol (open point O83, decided 2026-11-16).** The protocol asks for the least and lets a member go further: a network is run with its own accounts and its own management, and is open to others on equal terms; whether it becomes a company of its own is the member's choice; an isolated system is exempt. So the three countries show three arrangements. Urvania chose the full split: Ferrano Grid became a company of its own in 2007, beside the power station and the retail company. Bayvarra keeps one energy company with a ring-fenced network division. The island, connected to no one, is exempt and has one company that generates, distributes and supplies. No example document shows a Bayvarran or Lisavan energy company; this is backstory. The model is the EU's own path (accounts and management first, a separate company later, exemptions for small isolated systems), to be checked against the directives before it is cited (O93). Since 2011 Kelpstone Gas of Bayvarra sells gas by pipeline to Urvania's power station and to Ferrano Energy.
- **A common minimum for customers.** Because the customers of a protected supplier cannot go elsewhere, the members agreed minimum rules for utility bills. One of them: a bill is due 21 days after it is issued, in all three countries.

### Document numbers (D24)

- **Every issuer keeps its own numbers,** per kind of document, rising with time. The examples show a sample of each company's sequence: a distributor that issues some 2,800 invoices a year is near number 920 at the end of April; a farm is in its low two digits.
- **The style differs.** In Urvania an invoice is numbered within the year, and the year is part of the number, by its rules for value added tax. Large companies use plain numeric ranges from their business systems, one range per kind of document, that run on from year to year. Mid-sized companies use a prefix, the kind and the year. Small ones write the year and a number. Public bodies use file references.
- **A number says nothing by itself.** What a document is, and who issued it, is said by its elements.
- **A transaction has an identifier of its own.** The party that starts a collaboration (a buyer with its order, a contracting body with its procurement file) gives it an identifier, and every document of that transaction repeats it (`ProfileExecutionID`, D26). It links an order, its despatch advice and its invoice without anyone having to follow the references.

### The calendar (D27), current base

**The week.** Monday to Friday for offices and factories, Monday to Saturday for shops, every day for ports, carriers, terminals, border customs and the operations of utilities. The same week in all three countries.

**The seasons are those of the south:** December is midsummer and August midwinter. The island's cotton is picked from March to May.

**When the countries are out of step.** On 1 May Urvania and the island are closed and Bayvarra works; on Monday 4 May it is the other way round. On Upland State Day Marrowdale is closed and Lumeport is open. An order sent on the other's holiday is read a day later; a due date on a holiday moves to the next working day; a payment between two countries settles on a day both banking systems are open; a deadline in working days counts the working days of the party that set it. Ports, ships, aircraft and border customs work through all of it.

**Weeks (owner's question, 2026-11-16).** The week starts on Monday in all three countries, and weeks are numbered as ISO 8601 numbers them: week 1 is the week with the year's first Thursday. In 2026 week 1 runs from Monday 29 December 2025 to Sunday 4 January 2026, and the year has 53 weeks. UBL has no element for a week number: a document states a week as a period with a start date and an end date (Monday to Sunday), described by the code `WEEK` of the world's period list; a week number appears only in free text ("delivery in week 21"), and then as the ISO number. No document of the examples states a week or a week number today (the periods described by a code are all `MONTH`), so nothing changes; the rule is recorded for the first document that does (a weekly forecast, a delivery schedule).

**Time.** The mainland is one hour behind UTC and the island on UTC, all year; there is no summer time. Each country names its own legal time (Urvanian Time, Bayvarran Time, Lisavu Time); documents state offsets from UTC and use no abbreviations. A flight that leaves Lumeport at 07:00 and lands in Selanu at 10:00 has been two hours in the air.

For comparison: the United Kingdom has 8 public holidays, Italy 12, the richer countries about 11 on average.

### Utilities: how a bill is settled (D31)

A business with a meter read monthly (the shop, the factory, the telephone customer) is billed every month on its reading. A household pays on account: each April the utility estimates the year, and a twelfth of it is collected every month by direct debit; the meter is read once a year, the annual statement and the settlement invoice bill the actual year less what was paid, and a new estimate sets the next year's monthly amount. In Lumeport the water company also runs the district heating, so a household on the network has one annual account for water and heat. In Urvania the electricity bill carries the network charges of Ferrano Grid beside the energy of Ferrano Energy; in Bayvarra the water bill carries the city's sewage levy, collected for the city. The island's telephone company has an account on the Coral net.

Urvania's chamber issues an ATA carnet for them on 25 May, so that no tax is paid on the island; customs stamp its counterfoils as the samples go: out of Urvania, through Bayvarra and back on the carnet's transit vouchers, into and out of the island by air, and home. ### Recycled granulate by barge (D41)

Rinova Recovery in Ferrano turns the production waste of several factories, Valdara's among them, into polyethylene granulate, audited and certified for its recycled content by an accredited certification body. In April it sells 120 one-tonne bags to Rivermouth Packaging on Bayvarra's coast, carriage paid to Rivermouth's river quay, and ships them on Fiumella's barge down the river; the freight of exported goods is exempt from Urvania's VAT. Rivermouth makes stretch film and garment bags from it: the film wraps Valdara's bench pallets, the bags the textile mill's shirts.

### Production waste to recovery (D42)

Valdara bales the polyethylene film and offcuts its factory throws out, about 275 t a year, and sends them to Rinova Recovery across Ferrano, which recycles them. Urvania's waste law asks the producer to notify the waste stream for the year to the environment agency, which gives it a number, and the recovery facility to report each load it receives. Trasvia, registered with the agency as a waste carrier, brings about 54 bales a load; Rinova's weighbridge weighs the truck full and empty, about 38.2 t and 15.3 t, and Rinova bills the 22.9 t of waste.

### Slats from the sawmill (D43)

Marrowdale Sawmill in Upland State saws Valdara's bench slats to Valdara's drawing, 1800 × 95 × 38 mm, kiln-dried, and sells them by the bundle of 50. Once a quarter a truck takes 96 bundles on 24 heat-treated pallets across the border to the factory: enough slats for 960 benches. The February delivery is oak; from May the sawmill saws ash, as its felling in the managed forest moves to an ash stand, and Lumeway tells its customers. The sawmill also sells timber by the cubic metre to Brackenmere at home, free of sales tax against Brackenmere's manufacture certificate.

### Gas and electricity (D44)

Kelpstone Gas produces gas from a field about 80 km off Bayvarra's north coast, treats it at its plant near Gullhaven and sends it by pipeline about 200 km across the border, metered at a station on Bayvarra's side. In March it sells 36,800 MWh to Montebrio Power, a gas-fired station of about 40 MW near Serravona, and 2,400 MWh to Ferrano Energy for its gas customers; it declares the month's export in one declaration with a consignment per buyer, and each buyer declares its own import. Montebrio turns the gas into 18,400 MWh of electricity for Ferrano Energy, which also buys from Urvania's other stations; Ferrano Grid carries both to the customers.

### Ore from the mine (D45)

Under a supply agreement for 2026 Valdara calls off one train of iron ore a month from Serravona Mining, free carrier at the mine siding, and books Ferrano Rail Cargo's ore train under their framework contract. On 10 March, the first day of the agreed window, the mine loads 30 wagons, about 1,790 t of lump ore, weighed at the siding; the train runs about 180 km on the line past Ferrano into the factory's siding and arrives the same afternoon, which earns the carrier the bonus the plan promises, so that the factory can start the blend a day early. The mine deducts 1.5 % for moisture, as the agreement's clause says.
