Kelpstone Gas

Natural gas producer on Bayvarra's north coast

Kelpstone Gas

Kelpstone Gas produces natural gas from an offshore field off the north coast of Bayvarra and treats it at its plant at Gullhaven, a fishing village in the Northern Territory. Founded in 2011, it sells the gas by pipeline to Urvania and propane by road tanker in Bayvarra, and employs about 1,300 people.[1][2]

History

The company was founded in 2011 and began producing that year, when the pipeline first brought gas to Urvania; the Trade Accord's energy protocol of 2007 had opened the wholesale trade in gas between the three countries. Because its customers are Urvanian, it registered with Urvania's Codana Identification Agency in October 2012. It was founded as the company of the field's licence, and a share issue on the Lumeport Exchange paid for the platform. Its shares are listed there, and the Northern Territory's royalty fund is its largest shareholder, with about a fifth. The company and its field take their name from the Kelpstone, a kelp-grown rock off Gullhaven that the fishermen use as a seamark.

Timeline

  • 2012: begins selling propane, carried by road tanker by Gullhaven Tank Haulage

Operations

A subsea line brings the gas ashore from the production platform to the plant, which treats it to pipeline quality. The pipeline runs about 207 km across the border to Montebrio Power, with a branch of about 97 km to the city gate of Ferrano Grid for the customers of Ferrano Energy; the gas is measured at a metering station on Bayvarra's side of the border. The plant also separates propane from the field's gas and sells it by the tonne, weighed on its weighbridge; Gullhaven Tank Haulage carries it by road tanker, among others to the Coastal State Water Company, which burns it in winter in the peak boilers of Lumeport's district heating. The production platform of the Kelpstone field is Kelpstone A. The field yields about 0.6 billion m³ of gas a year, about 6 TWh, and the plant about 50,000 t of propane; about 15 billion m³ remain, enough for about 25 years. The field was developed for more than Urvania's needs, and the company looks for further buyers of its gas.
