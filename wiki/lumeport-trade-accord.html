Lumeport Trade Accord

Free trade area of Urvania, Bayvarra and Lisavu

Lumeport Trade Accord
Signed1994, in Lumeport
In force1 January 1996
MembersUrvania, Bayvarra, Lisavu
SeatLumeport

The Lumeport Trade Accord is the free trade area of Urvania, Bayvarra and Lisavu. It was signed in Lumeport in 1994 and entered into force on 1 January 1996, turning the Lumeport Agreement of 1974 between Bayvarra and Lisavu into a free trade area of all three countries. Its council, the Lumeport Trade Accord Council, sits in Lumeport.[1][2]

Background

In 1974 Bayvarra and Lisavu signed the Lumeport Agreement: the island's cotton entered Bayvarra free of duty, a regular shipping service linked the two, and the island's coral was tied to the lume. Urvania opened its protected economy in the early 1990s and joined its two neighbours in the Accord.

Provisions

Goods of Accord origin cross between the members free of duty. The three currencies, the ferra, the lume and the coral, stand at a fixed parity. Each member keeps its own taxes: the island stopped charging duty on its partners' goods and kept its import consumption tax, which falls on every import whatever its origin. Carriage between the members follows the Accord's uniform rules, and the members share code lists for their electronic business documents.

Protocols

Later protocols extended the Accord. The procurement protocol of 1999 opened the members' public tenders to suppliers from the partners under common rules. The payment protocol of 2003 set up the payment link between the three banking systems and the card schemes used across the Accord. The energy protocol of 2007 opened the wholesale trade in gas and electricity between producers, networks and suppliers, and asks that a network be run with its own accounts and management and be open to others on equal terms. The protocol of 2014 on electronic business documents gave the members a common agreement for exchanging them and the code lists they share.

Public services

The Accord's chapter on public services, part of the Accord since 1994, keeps the supply of electricity, gas, heat from a network, water and telephone to end customers a national matter: each member keeps its own utilities, public or under national licence, and no utility of one member supplies end customers in another; the energy protocol of 2007 brought gas under the chapter. The chapter sets a common minimum for the customers: a bill is due 21 days after it is issued; every bill carries a statement of consumption, with each connection, the meter readings and the period, and other parties' charges on lines of their own; meters are read at least once a year and payments on account are settled each year; a household is cut off for non-payment only after written notice of at least 14 days; and each member names a body that hears customers' complaints. Every utility of the three members is public today: owned by the state, by a state of the federation, or run as a government department.
