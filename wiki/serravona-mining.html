Serravona Mining

Urvanian iron ore mine on the flank of the massif

Serravona Mining works an underground iron ore mine on the lower flank of the volcanic massif in the north-west of Urvania, with its address at Fucinalta. It was founded in 1958 and employs about 987 people.

History

The company was founded in 1958, under the British administration, as a joint company of the administration and Ferrano's iron merchants, to work the ore at Serravona. The first ore left the new mine on 4 December 1958, Saint Barbara's day, which the company chose because she is the miners' patron in the Italian tradition. In 1963 the new state bought out the merchants, and the mine became one of the pillars of the protected economy, beside state energy and the country's own manufacturers. Urvania kept it when it opened its economy in the 1990s: the company is wholly owned by the state, which holds the ore as a national asset.

Operations

The mine is entered by an adit in the hillside at about 720 m, 29 km from the mining village of Serravona. A small crushing and screening plant and a spoil heap stand beside it; the hillside around it stays green. The ore line is laid up to the portal, where the wagons are loaded and weighed at the mine's own siding, and runs down to the works of Valdara Industries in Ferrano: a train runs about 159 km from the mine siding to the works siding. Its reserves last about 40 years at the present rate of one train a month.

Trade

The mine sells lump ore with about 62 % iron to Valdara under a yearly supply agreement: one train a month, free carrier at the mine siding, with a deduction for moisture. A train of 30 wagons carries about 1,790 t, hauled by Ferrano Rail Cargo.
