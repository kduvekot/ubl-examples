Lisavu is a volcanic island about 1,080 km east of Lumeport, with about 60,000 people. Its capital and only town is Selanu, on a sheltered bay of the east coast. Lisavan and English are spoken. Its currency is the Coral (XLC), counted in thousandths.

Geography

The island is about 116 by 85 km and 7,100 km², an eroded volcano that rises to about 1,810 m some 25 km inland from the west coast and falls to the east. The west coast faces the westerly wind and the ocean swell: cliffs, bays where the land has slid into the sea, stacks and islets, and no port. Ships round a cape to the lee side, where the port and Selanu lie. The airport stands on the plain about 10 km north of the town, and the cotton grows on a plain of about 690 km² in the dry north-east, in the island's rain shadow.

History

The Lisavans lived on the island before the settlers came; the village on the sheltered bay gave Selanu its name. In the 19th century English settlers laid out cotton estates on the dry north-eastern plain, at Lintwell and Bollworth, and built their town, Port Wrenfield, on the wharf beside the village. In 1974 the island signed the Lumeport Agreement with Bayvarra: its cotton entered Bayvarra free of duty, a regular shipping service linked the two, and the coral was tied to the lume. In 1994 it joined the Lumeport Trade Accord.

Economy

Cotton is the island's export. Lisavu has no general sales tax: customs collects an import consumption tax on every import, whatever its origin, and a duty on goods from outside the Accord.
