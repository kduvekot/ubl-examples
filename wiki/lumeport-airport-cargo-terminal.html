Lumeport Airport Cargo Terminal

Air cargo terminal at Lumeport airport, Bayvarra

Lumeport Airport Cargo Terminal runs the air cargo terminal of Lumeport airport, Bayvarra, on Airport Road outside Lumeport. Founded in 1983 for air freight to the island of Lisavu, it handles urgent and sensitive shipments overseas and employs about 85 people.

History

Lumeport airport began in 1937 as a federal flying-boat and mail station on the estuary; the land airport on Airport Road opened in 1952, and its present runway in 1971. The federal airport authority built the terminal in 1983, when air freight to Lisavu outgrew the passenger sheds, a year after the island's airfield at Lintwell had opened to flights from Lumeport. The authority owns it still.

Operations

The terminal works around the clock. It accepts cargo from forwarders such as Bayforth Forwarding and loads it onto the freighter of Kelaro Air Cargo, which flies about 1,080 km to Selanu airport, where Selanu Airport Cargo receives it. Cargo that reaches the terminal after a flight's cut-off waits for the next flight. It handles dangerous goods, such as batteries for the island's telecom mast that may fly only on cargo aircraft, and the cold chain and radioactive packages that Quillmoor Pharma Distribution sends to Selanu General Hospital. The terminal has a shed of about 5,000 m², with a cold room at 2 to 8 °C, a room at 15 to 25 °C, a freezer, a cage for dangerous goods and a store for radioactive packages, and handles about 12,000 t a year, about a third of it on Kelaro's flights. Lumeport is the mainland's main airport, used by Urvanians as well: Bayvarra's national airline flies from it to a few destinations abroad, foreign airlines fly in from their hubs, and Kelaro is the only airline to Selanu, a route the national airline gave up in 1987.
