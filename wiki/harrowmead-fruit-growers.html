Harrowmead Fruit Growers

Fruit growers' cooperative at Harrowmead, Bayvarra

Harrowmead Fruit Growers

Harrowmead Fruit Growers is a cooperative owned by the fruit growers of the slopes above Harrowmead, in the wetter west of Upland State, Bayvarra. Founded in 1958, it grades, packs and cold-stores its members' apples and pears and sells them at home and overseas. It employs about 160 people.

History

The growers founded the cooperative in 1958, after a year of glut in which apples sold below what they cost to grow, to grade, store and sell their fruit together.

Facilities

Its packhouse and cold store stand on Orchard Road at the edge of Harrowmead, a market town of the western wheat land founded by British settlers in the 1850s; the orchards lie on the slopes above the town.

Operations

Its members grow Gala and Granny Smith apples and Packham's Triumph pears, packed in cartons of 18 kg and graded under the Lumeport Trade Accord's marketing standard for fresh fruit. Gala and the pears are picked in February and March and kept in its cold store; Granny Smith, the late variety, is picked in April. Lisavu is among its markets: it sells a reefer container of fruit to Hafefo Fresh Produce in Selanu, carriage and insurance paid to the port, and has Bayforth Forwarding book the reefer on Tidewell Ocean Lines, bring it pre-cooled to the packhouse and insure the fruit. Before loading, the Plant Health Service of the Federal Department of Agriculture of Bayvarra inspects the fruit at the packhouse for the pests the island keeps out. The cooperative has about 85 member growers, with about 1,400 ha of orchards. At home it sells to Bayvarra's grocers and to the markets of Lumeport and Marrowdale.

Products

  • Apples Gala, carton 18 kg, count 100
  • Apples Granny Smith, carton 18 kg, count 100
  • Pears Packham's Triumph, carton 18 kg, count 80
