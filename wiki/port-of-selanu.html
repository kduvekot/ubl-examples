Port of Selanu

Seaport of Selanu, Lisavu

The Port of Selanu runs the seaport of Selanu, Lisavu: its berths, its container yard and its vessel traffic. It is a company owned by the state, formed in 1972, and employs about 70 people.[1][2]

History

The island's harbour office was turned into a company owned by the island's state in 1972, so that the port would be ready for the regular shipping service that the Lumeport Agreement with Bayvarra, then being negotiated, was to bring; the Agreement was signed on 18 March 1974. Its offices are on Wharf Road in Selanu.

Timeline

  • 1972: the island's harbour office made a company of the state, ahead of the Lumeport Agreement
  • 18 March 1974: the Lumeport Agreement is signed, bringing the regular shipping service from Lumeport
  • 1989: opens its branch at the airport, Selanu Airport Cargo

Operations

Selanu is the island's only port, on a sheltered bay of the east coast, out of the westerly swell. Ships from Lumeport, among them the weekly service of Tidewell Ocean Lines, sail about 1,200 km to reach it in about 48 hours. The port has two deep-water berths, 10 m deep, enough for the feeders of 1,000 containers, and a container yard for about 800 of them. Its own pilots bring ships in by daylight only, so a ship that arrives in the dark waits under way and berths at first light. The container yard has a reefer rack where refrigerated containers wait on the port's power until they are collected, at a daily charge. The port reports discharged containers to the shippers' forwarders, among them Bayforth Forwarding.

Harbour parks and branch

The port keeps harbour parks, for which Velumi Trading imports park benches of Valdara Industries and their spare frames. Its branch Selanu Airport Cargo runs the cargo terminal at the airport on the plain north of the town.
