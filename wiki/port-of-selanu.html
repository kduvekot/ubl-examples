Port of Selanu

Seaport of Selanu, Lisavu

The Port of Selanu runs the seaport of Selanu, Lisavu: its berths, its container yard and its vessel traffic. It is a company owned by the state, formed in 1972, and employs about 150 people.

History

The port was formed as a company in 1972, ahead of the Lumeport Agreement of 1974 between Bayvarra and Lisavu, which brought a regular shipping service to the island. Its offices are on Wharf Road in Selanu.

Operations

Selanu is the island's only port, on a sheltered bay of the east coast, out of the westerly swell. Ships from Lumeport, among them the weekly service of Tidewell Ocean Lines, sail about 1,200 km to reach it in about 48 hours. Selanu's pilots bring ships in by daylight only, so a ship that arrives in the dark waits under way and berths at first light. The container yard has a reefer rack where refrigerated containers wait on the port's power until they are collected, at a daily charge. The port reports discharged containers to the shippers' forwarders, among them Bayforth Forwarding.

Harbour parks and branch

The port keeps harbour parks, for which Velumi Trading imports park benches of Valdara Industries and their spare frames. Its branch Selanu Airport Cargo runs the cargo terminal at the airport on the plain north of the town.
