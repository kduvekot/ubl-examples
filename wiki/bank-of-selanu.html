Bank of Selanu

Bank in Selanu, Lisavu

Bank of Selanu is the oldest bank of Lisavu, at Reef House on Reef Street in Selanu. Founded in 1878 by the island's own leaders, it holds the accounts of the island's buyers, producers, carriers and brokers and makes and receives their payments; it employs about 190 people.[1][2]

History

The Lisavan rulers founded the bank in 1878 together with the cotton estates and the merchants of Selanu, to finance the crop and the island's trade overseas: they meant to handle that trade themselves. It kept its place when the island came under British protection in 1884, and it has been the island's own bank ever since, through full self-government in 1962 and the Lumeport Agreement with Bayvarra in 1974, which tied the coral to the lume. At self-government in 1962 the rulers' founding share passed to the island's state, which holds about 40 % of the bank; the rest belongs to the successors of the estates and the merchant houses and to island firms. Since 1974 the bank's articles let only islanders and island companies hold its shares.

Operations

It has a main branch and a market branch in Selanu. Its customers include Velumi Trading, Selanu Cotton Ginnery, Lisavu Cotton Farm, Selanu Telecom, Tevaro Customs Brokers, Nalevi Warehousing and Hafefo Fresh Produce. The island's clearing, Coral net, settles tokenised deposits on the bank's ledger. It went live on 7 July 2023, Island Day, in place of the island's cheque clearing, and the island's other banks settle on it as well. Payments to and from the mainland run over the Trade Accord's payment link; when an island customer pays in lume, the bank changes its coral at the fixed parity. One of the payments that brings money to the island is the Lumeport Textile Mill's payment for the ginnery's cotton. The island's banks run the Coralcard, the debit card in coral.
