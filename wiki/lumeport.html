Lumeport

Capital and port city of Bayvarra

Lumeport is the capital and port city of Bayvarra, with about 400,000 people, on a bay at the narrows of the estuary. It gave its name to the Lumeport Trade Accord, signed there in 1994, and is the seat of its council.[1][2]

Geography

The town stands on the shore of the bay where the estuary of the Runmek narrows before it opens to the sea; the narrows command the entry to the river. A brook from the low hills west of the centre runs through the town to the bay and gave it its first fresh water. The deep-water quays lie at the inner basin behind the narrows.

History

The colony's seat

In the late 1830s the settlers' company kept a trading station at the narrows. When Britain annexed the coast on 29 November 1841 it fortified the station and laid out the town beside it, and as the river trade grew Lumeport took over from Larkmouth as capital in 1852.

Port city

Government Hill gained its government buildings, churches and banks in the 19th century, and the deep-water quays and their railway came between the 1870s and 1914. In 1899 the town became the capital of the federation.

Agreement and Accord

The Lumeport Agreement with Lisavu, signed in the town on 18 March 1974, brought the textile mill of 1976, built to spin the island's duty-free cotton; the Lumeport Trade Accord, signed here in 1994, made the town the seat of its council.
