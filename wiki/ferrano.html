Capital of Urvania

Ferrano (Urvanian Ruldraz) is the capital of Urvania, with about 600,000 people. It stands where the northern valley meets the Runmek, just below the rapids that end navigation: the country's works, its rail hub and its river port.[1][2]

Geography

The town lies on the terraces of the north bank, at the foot of the rapids, where the barges of the lower river turn. A road bridge, about 20 m above the river, links the farmland south of the river with the town; the suburb of Oltreponte grew beyond it. The railway runs north up the valley towards the mine and east towards the border with Bayvarra.

History

Ruldraz

Ruldraz was the Urvanians' town before the Europeans came, where the valleys meet below the rapids of the Runmek: its lanes, market and old bridgehead are the old town. A Tuscan trading post of 1609–10 at the landing below the rapids left the walled warehouse court of Borgo Toscano.

The protectorate

The treaty of protection, signed in the town on 4 September 1840, made it the protectorate's seat under its Italian name, Ferrano, and the city keeps that day as its Founders' Day, though Ruldraz is older. The Sardinian and then Italian protectorate laid out Città Nuova on the higher terrace, and the railway and the first works came in the 1880s.

Since 1941

The British administration of 1941 to 1962 added a garden suburb. After independence the town grew along its roads and its railway, with the works and depots of the protected economy and, from 1996, of the Accord.

Districts

District Grew Character
Ruldraz (the old town) before 1609 The Urvanian town on the terrace of the north bank where the northern valley meets the river below the rapids: its lanes, the market and the old bridgehead.
Borgo Toscano 1609 to the 18th century The Tuscan trading post of 1609-10 by the landing at the foot of the rapids: a walled warehouse court and a chapel; the river quays grew from it.
Città Nuova about 1840 to 1900 The Sardinian and then Italian new town on the higher terrace north of the old town: a grid of streets, the residency, the barracks, the churches and the arcaded main street.
Scalo 1880s to 1930s The railway station, its yards and the first works beside the line north of the centre (the iron foundry, later the bench works).
Villaggio Inglese 1941 to 1962 The garden suburb of the British administration's officials and families on the hill north-west of the centre.
Zona Industriale after 1962 Industry and logistics along the railway east of the centre: the works, the intermodal hub, the depots and warehouses of the protected economy and of the Accord's trade.
Borgo Nord after 1962 Flats and houses along the road north, up the valley.
Oltreponte after 1962 The suburb across the river bridge, along the road south.
Quartiere Est after 1962 Housing along the main road east, towards the border.
