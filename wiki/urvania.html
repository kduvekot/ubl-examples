Urvania

Country on the west of the mainland, member of the Lumeport Trade Accord

Urvania is the western of the two countries of the mainland, with about 1.2 million people. It holds the volcanic massif of the north-west, the green western hills and all the land south of the Runmek. Its capital is Ferrano, the old Urvanian town of Ruldraz below the rapids. Urvania is bilingual: Urvanian and English are its official languages. Its currency is the ferra (XUF).[1][2]

Geography

Urvania covers about 90,500 km². A volcanic massif in the north-west, the Mukrir (Italian Massiccio del Padre, father), rises to about 1,920 m at the Cima Bianca; the Runmek rises on it at about 1,510 m and runs through a rift valley to the sea. Just above Ferrano it falls through rapids, which end navigation; below them the river carries barges to the estuary. The western hills and the land south of the river are green and hilly, wet from the westerly winds. From the border crossing the Runmek forms the border with Bayvarra down to the sea; north of the crossing the border follows the watershed, so that Urvania keeps the whole valley of the largest plateau river, the Bruna.

Urvania lies in the belt of the South Atlantic high, about 30° south. Its summers, from November to March, are dry and sunny, with light north-east winds and about 23 °C on the coast; its winters, from May to September, bring the westerly winds, the fronts and the clouds, at about 17 °C on the coast. The rainiest months are March to June, the autumn and the start of winter. The westerlies drop their rain on the massif and the western hills, so the west and the high ground are wet and the south-east, around Saline Brune, is the driest and sunniest land. The massif's summit, about 12 °C colder than the coast, has snow in some winters.

The Urvanians call their heartland, the rift valley of the Runmek, the Gamir (the valley). The wet hills of the west are the Colli Piovosi (Urvanian Skarbek, rain), the sunny hills of the south-east the Colli del Sole (Vidrek, sun), and the rugged south coast, without a deep-water port, the Costa Ligure, after the Ligurian fishermen who settled at Marina Ligure.

Government

Urvania is a parliamentary republic under its constitution of 1962. The president, its head of state, is elected by parliament for seven years. Parliament has a Chamber of Deputies and a Senate elected by the provinces, and the government, a prime minister with a council of ministers, answers to it.

History

Before the Europeans came, the Urvanians farmed the wet valley of the Runmek and the hills west of it; their town, Ruldraz, grew where the valleys meet, below the rapids. In 1609–10 a voyage from Tuscany reached Ruldraz and founded a small trading post, which lasted some decades and faded in the 18th century; it left Ferrano's quarter Borgo Toscano and the chapel of San Ranieri del Guado.

In 1840 the Sardinian corvette Euridice sailed up the river to Ruldraz. The Urvanian rulers signed a treaty of protection with Sardinia at Ruldraz on 4 September 1840, and in 1842 Forte Euridice was built at the confluence of the Runmek and the Bruna. A boundary convention of 1857 set the border with the British colony to the east. In 1861 the Kingdom of Italy inherited the protectorate, and Urvania stayed an Italian territory until 1941, when British forces took it and put it under British administration; Italy renounced it in the peace treaty of 1947. On 17 June 1951, after years of asking, the Urvanians won official status for their language beside English, in the courts, the schools and the administration; the day is kept as Language Day.

Urvania became independent on 9 March 1962, by an agreement with Britain that ended its administration, bilingual in Urvanian and English. A constituent assembly elected that spring adopted the constitution on 14 October 1962. Both days are public holidays: Independence Day and Constitution Day. The British had kept the Italian place and street names, and the country kept a continental cast in its institutions, among them its value added tax. It built a protected economy around state mining, state energy and its own manufacturers, and opened it in the early 1990s. In 1994 it joined Bayvarra and Lisavu in the Lumeport Trade Accord.

Economy

Urvania's economy grew around the mine, the energy companies and the works of Ferrano, among them Valdara Industries, the bench maker founded in the year of independence. Its energy market is unbundled: a power station produces electricity, a grid operator runs the networks and a retail company supplies the customers. The country levies a value added tax at every stage, deducted by businesses and not charged on exports.

Urvania uses about 3 TWh of electricity a year, with a peak of about 550 MW. About two-thirds comes from water: the Nevola hydro scheme in the massif, a dam that holds the Lago della Nevola and a power station of about 300 MW, built from 1956 to 1961, and run-of-river plants on the upper Runmek and the Pastora of about 120 MW together, built in the 1960s. The sun gives about a tenth: the Saline Brune solar park of about 150 MW, on the dry salt-marsh edge behind the south coast, opened in 2018, and the roofs of homes and farms, which sell their surplus. When the sun shines the reservoirs hold back their water for the evening. Montebrio Power covers the peaks and the dry years, and the rest is bought from Bayvarra on the Accord's wholesale market.

Languages

Urvanian and English are official. Place and street names are the Italian ones, which count as English; Urvanian gives every place its own name too, and addresses carry both.
