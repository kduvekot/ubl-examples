Selanu Telecom

Telephone and data provider of Lisavu

Selanu Telecom is the telephone and data provider of Lisavu, on Mast Hill Road in Selanu. It became a company in 1993 and employs about 110 people.[1][2]

History

The island's telephone department began in 1931, when a radio telephone link to Lumeport opened. Selanu Telecom was that department until 1993, when it was turned into a company. It is owned by the state and entered in the business register like any company.

Timeline

  • 1931: the island's telephone department opens a radio telephone link to Lumeport
  • 1979: a satellite earth station joins the radio link
  • 1993: the telephone department made a company owned by the state
  • 2012: a submarine fibre cable to Lumeport, landed near Cape Sihato
  • 2016: fibre in Selanu

Operations

It supplies telephone lines, data lines and mobile data to the island's customers and bills its business customers every month for its own service. Its customers' mobile data is also carried on partner networks abroad, which it charges per gigabyte, as the Lumeport Trade Accord has no rule on roaming. Under the Accord's chapter on public services the supply stays national, so Selanu Telecom is the island's only provider. Its network stores supply the mast on Mast Hill, for which it buys back-up batteries and other equipment from Saltmere Engineering in Bayvarra.

Network

The network has about 19,000 fixed lines and twelve mobile sites besides the mast on Mast Hill, covering about 95 % of the island's inhabited land with 4G, and fibre in Selanu since 2016. The island's first link to the mainland was the radio telephone of 1931; a satellite earth station followed in 1979, and since 2012 a submarine fibre cable runs to Lumeport, landed near Cape Sihato, with satellite as its back-up. Its roaming partners are the mobile networks of Bayvarra and Urvania and a satellite network for ships at sea.
