Selanu Cotton Ginnery

Cotton ginnery at Bollworth, Lisavu

Selanu Cotton Ginnery
TypeIsland limited company
Founded1936-06-10
SeatBollworth, Lisavu

Selanu Cotton Ginnery gins the island's seed cotton at Bollworth, on the dry north-eastern plain of Lisavu, and exports the lint, the island's main export. It was founded in 1936 and employs about 64 people.

History

The estates and the Bank of Selanu built the ginnery together in 1936, when cotton was already the island's old export, grown on the estates that English settlers laid out on the plain in the 1860s, to gin all the island's cotton in one plant at Bollworth, where the bales were landed for the ships. It carries the capital's name because the island's lint sells abroad as Selanu cotton. It took over the ginning from the estates' own gins and buys from the estates and the island's small farms alike. Since the Lumeport Agreement of 1974 the island's cotton enters Bayvarra free of duty.

Operations

Under a season agreement the ginnery advances each farm, such as Lisavu Cotton Farm, a quarter of the value of its expected delivery in January, while the crop grows. When the farms bring their seed cotton after the harvest, from March to May, the ginnery weighs and grades it, settles each delivery for the farm itself, and gins it into compressed bales of about 227 kg. The lint is classed by the Selanu Chamber of Commerce and certified free of pests by the Department of Agriculture of Lisavu. About 320 farms deliver their seed cotton to it, a dozen of them old estates.

Trade

Its buyer is Lumeport Textile Mill, which buys the lint free on board Selanu; the bales cross to Lumeport on the ships of Tidewell Ocean Lines. All its lint goes to the mill; the cottonseed is sold on the island as cattle feed.

Products

  • Cotton lint, compressed bale
