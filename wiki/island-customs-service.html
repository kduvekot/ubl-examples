Island Customs Service

Customs service of Lisavu, at Selanu

Island Customs Service
SeatSelanu, Lisavu

The Island Customs Service is the customs service of Lisavu, a public body at the port and the airport of Selanu. It clears the island's imports and exports, collects the import consumption tax, and learns what a ship carries before it arrives.

  3. Traders and carnets

History

The service grew from the harbour dues office of the island's rulers in the cotton years. Under the British protectorate it was organised as a customs service in 1885, and it has been the island's own since self-government in 1962. It reports to the island's Treasury Department. Its head office is the import office on Wharf Road, and it keeps a desk at the airport.

Operations

Its import office works around the clock. The island has no general sales tax: customs collects an import consumption tax of 10 % on the landed value of every import, food included, and a customs duty on goods from outside the Lumeport Trade Accord. Since the Accord came into force in 1996 the island charges no duty on its partners' goods. Medicines supplied to the public hospital are free of the tax. Its export office clears the island's cotton bales, which leave through the Port of Selanu.

Traders and carnets

Importers often declare through the licensed customs broker Tevaro Customs Brokers, and a wholesaler may declare fresh food while the ship is still at sea so that it is released on discharge. Goods shown at the Selanu Chamber of Commerce's trade fair can enter under an ATA carnet without the island's tax, provided they leave again by the carnet's time limit. The service has a staff of about 60.
