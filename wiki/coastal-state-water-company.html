Coastal State Water Company

Water utility of Coastal State and Lumeport's district heating

The Coastal State Water Company supplies drinking water in Coastal State, Bayvarra, to shops and households, and runs the district heating of Lumeport. It is a state-owned utility, founded in 1954, and employs about 410 people.

History

Coastal State set up the company in 1954, after the dry summer of 1953, when Lumeport's wells ran low, and dammed a reservoir in the hills west of the city; it owns the company still. Under the Lumeport Trade Accord the supply of water to end customers stays a national matter: each country keeps its own utilities, and none supplies customers in another.

Operations

The company's water works stand in Westside, on the estuary shore west of Lumeport's centre, and its head office on Reservoir Road. It bills businesses such as Brightmarket Stores every month on their meter readings; households pay a twelfth of the year's estimate each month by direct debit, and the meters are read once a year, at the end of March. With its water bill it collects the city of Lumeport's sewage levy on the city's behalf. A household on the heating network has one annual account for water, the levy and heat, with a correction when the return water comes back too warm.

District heating

The peak boilers of the district heating burn propane in winter. The company buys it from Kelpstone Gas, whose tankers of Gullhaven Tank Haulage drive about 515 km from the gas plant at Gullhaven and pump it into the storage at the heating plant on Reservoir Road. Its greenhouse-gas report asks its suppliers' carriers for the emissions of the whole round trip.
