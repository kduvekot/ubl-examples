Coastal State Water Company

Water utility of Coastal State and Lumeport's district heating

Coastal State Water Company
TypeState-owned company limited by shares

SeatLumeport, Bayvarra

The Coastal State Water Company supplies drinking water in Coastal State, Bayvarra, to shops and households, and runs the district heating of Lumeport. It is a state-owned utility, founded in 1954, and employs about 410 people.[1][2]

History

Coastal State set up the company in 1954, after the dry summer of 1953, when Lumeport's wells ran low, and dammed a reservoir in the hills west of the city; it owns the company still. Under the Lumeport Trade Accord's chapter on public services, the supply stays national.

Timeline

  • 1954: set up by Coastal State after the dry summer of 1953; dams a reservoir in the hills west of Lumeport
  • 1966: begins Lumeport's district heating network, oil-fired, finished in 1970
  • 2006: a wood-chip plant takes over the base heat
  • 2012: the peak boilers turn to propane from Kelpstone Gas

Operations

The company's water works stand in Westside, on the estuary shore west of Lumeport's centre, and its head office on Reservoir Road. It bills businesses such as Brightmarket Stores every month on their meter readings; households pay a twelfth of the year's estimate each month by direct debit, and the meters are read once a year, at the end of March. With its water bill it collects the city of Lumeport's sewage levy on the city's behalf. A household on the heating network has one annual account for water, the levy and heat, with a correction when the return water comes back too warm. Most of its water comes from the reservoir in the western hills; Lumeport's old wells in the estuary gravels are kept as a reserve for dry years. It has about 160,000 connections and serves about 480,000 people in Lumeport and the coast towns.

District heating

The district heating network was built from 1966 to 1970 for the Government Hill quarter and the flats of Westside, at first with oil boilers. Since 2006 a wood-chip plant gives the base heat, burning offcuts of Marrowdale Sawmill and thinnings of the Upland State Forest. The peak boilers have burned propane in winter since 2012. The company buys it from Kelpstone Gas, whose tankers of Gullhaven Tank Haulage drive about 515 km from the gas plant at Gullhaven and pump it into the storage at the heating plant on Reservoir Road. Its greenhouse-gas report asks its suppliers' carriers for the emissions of the whole round trip.
