Rivers and watersheds

The rivers, basins and watersheds of the mainland and Lisavu

The mainland drains to the sea by many rivers, of which 36 have a basin of 1,000 km² or more; Lisavu's streams are short and steep, and 4 of them drain more than 500 km². The largest river is the Runmek, 471 km long, whose basin of 53,248 km² covers about 33 % of the mainland.[1][2]

The mainland

The Runmek rises on the volcanic massif in Urvania's north-west at about 1,510 m and runs south-east through the rift valley to the estuary at Lumeport. Its tributaries come from the massif, from the plateau to the north-east and from the southern hills; the largest is the Bruna, which joins it at Forte Euridice. From the massif the Pastora falls to the north coast and the Vallerna and the Grigiola to the west coast. Bayvarra's north drains to the north coast by the Shingle, the Brackwater and the Scarrow, its north-east by the Sallow, and the dry east by the Lark, the second river of the mainland. The southern hills send only short rivers to the south coast, among them the Colomba and the Genovina.

Watersheds and the border

North of the border crossing near Forte Euridice the border between Urvania and Bayvarra follows the watershed: Urvania holds the basins of the rivers that join the Runmek at or above the crossing, the Bruna's whole valley among them, and of the north-western coast; Bayvarra holds the land that drains to the Runmek below the crossing, to the rest of the north coast and to the east. From the crossing down to the estuary the Runmek itself is the border.

Lisavu

The island's streams run outward from its volcano, short and steep. The Vokila drains the largest basin and reaches the north coast in the bay of Natahoma; the Makete, the longest, reaches the south coast at Linukaha; the Tapetu falls from near the summit to the south-west coast at Nihekemo; the Kanipi runs to the east coast north of Selanu.
