How UBL helps

Customs needs to know what crosses the border, for whom and how. UBL customs declarations reuse the parties, goods and transport details already present in the commercial and transport documents, so the declarant does not have to collect them again.

In this world it sends 0 and receives 8 documents, and is named in 2 documents between other parties. They are listed per flow below.

Finished goods by truck across the land border (Urvania → Bayvarra)

The distributor in Bayvarra buys 96 park benches and 12 spare side frames from the manufacturer in Urvania, free carrier (FCA) at the manufacturer's 3PL warehouse, and books the road carrier itself. The manufacturer confirms the order with the new casting of the side frame, at the same price; Urvania's chamber of commerce certifies the goods' preferential origin; the manufacturer clears them for export, the distributor for import. At goods-in a bench seems to be short and a carton of frames over; the next morning the recount finds the bench's carton on the pallet of spare frames, and the distributor withdraws its first receipt advice and sends a corrected one: everything ordered arrived. The carrier invoices the transport to the distributor, the manufacturer invoices the goods, and the distributor pays across the border.

Receives

  • Export Customs Declaration from Valdara Industries: Goods leave Urvania: the manufacturer, as exporter, declares them to the customs office at the land border. See the example document.

    In it as CustomsDeclaration/CustomsParty.

    Also mentions: Lumeway Distribution as CustomsDeclaration/ConsigneeParty; Trasvia Haulage as CustomsDeclaration/Shipment/Consignment/CarrierParty.

Containers by rail under the Accord's common transit (Urvania → Bayvarra)

For full container loads Valdara sells Lumeway 192 benches delivered at place (DAP) Lumeway's rail siding in Marrowdale. The seller arranges and pays the transport: it books Ferrano Rail Cargo under their framework contract for container slots. The carrier's tractor brings the two packed 40-foot containers from Castrano's warehouse to the intermodal hub at Ferrano, a pre-carriage by road of the same contract, and the hub lifts them onto the container train FRC 4417; the consignment note follows the Accord's uniform rules for international carriage by rail, modelled on the CIM. Urvania's rail infrastructure manager, which runs the tracks and the train paths since the state separated them from the train operators, reports the train's progress to the carrier. The goods travel under the Accord's common transit, which Valdara lodges the evening before departure, to Bayvarra customs' inland office at Marrowdale, where Lumeway declares the import on 15 April and claims the Accord preference. Lumeway empties the first container the next day; the second waits until 22 April, because the siding's fork-lift is in repair, and runs two days past the five days' free time. Ferrano Rail Cargo charges the demurrage to Valdara in a debit note to its freight invoice, and Valdara passes it on to Lumeway, which bears a delay in unloading under DAP, in a debit note to its goods invoice: the world's debit notes.

Receives

  • Transit Customs Declaration from Valdara Industries: The goods travel under the Accord's common transit from Urvania's office of departure at the hub to Bayvarra customs' inland office at Marrowdale, the office of destination; the manufacturer, as holder of the procedure, lodges the declaration the evening before the train leaves. See the example document.

    In it as CustomsParty.

    Also mentions: Lumeway Distribution as ConsigneeParty; Ferrano Rail Cargo as Shipment/Consignment/CarrierParty.

Recycled plastic granulate by river barge (Urvania → Bayvarra)

Rinova Recovery in Ferrano recovers polyethylene from the production waste of several factories, Valdara's among them (the waste flow), and sells 120 tonnes of granulate in 120 jumbo bags of one tonne to Rivermouth Packaging on Bayvarra's coast, carriage paid to (CPT) Rivermouth's river quay: the seller books the barge and pays the freight, the buyer takes the risk once the bags are on board. Before the order Rinova sends its product information for the quarter, with the recycled-content certificate of an accredited certification body. The bags are loaded on Monday 20 April and sail down the river by day; the freight of exported goods is exempt from Urvania's value added tax. Rivermouth turns the granulate into stretch film and garment bags (the packaging flow).

A public tender (Bayvarra)

The contracting authority buys park benches for the city's parks under procurement law, in two lots: the benches and their installation. The notices are published through the government information service; the manufacturer tenders alone, with an engineering firm as its subcontractor for the installation, backed by a bank guarantee; a furniture maker tenders wooden benches, backed by pledged government bonds. After an electronic auction and the award the authority orders the first release under the framework contract; the manufacturer delivers and invoices the benches, the subcontractor installs them, the authority accepts the release, and the installation is invoiced once accepted.

Receives

Samples to a trade fair on the island (temporary export)

The manufacturer shows two sample benches at the Selanu Chamber of Commerce's trade fair in early June and brings them back. An ATA carnet issued by Urvania's chamber of commerce lets them into the island without its import consumption tax; customs stamp its counterfoils as the samples leave Urvania, cross Bayvarra on its transit vouchers, and enter and leave the island by air. The island's re-exportation voucher goes astray, so after the time limit the island's chamber asks Urvania's chamber for proof of re-exportation, reminds it a month later, and receives the proof; the claim for the island's tax falls away.

Wood: slats for the benches and timber for furniture (Bayvarra → Urvania, and at home)

Marrowdale Sawmill in Upland State makes Valdara's bench slats to Valdara's drawing: it saws and kiln-dries ash from the managed forest, modifies it at about 212 °C, planes it to 1800 × 95 × 45 mm and sells the slats by the bundle of 50. It modifies the ash in a thermal modification kiln of its own; in the southern summer, when that kiln is full with its own decking and cladding, it has the slats modified under contract at Tostara Wood Modification in Conciaro, near Ferrano, and names that processor to Valdara, whose approval of the supplier covers it. Valdara asks for a quotation, which the sawmill gives under Valdara's purchasing terms, for the modified ash with kiln-dried oak of the same size as a priced alternative, and orders 96 bundles of the ash delivered at its factory (DAP). The sawmill fills the February delivery from the season stock of slats it had modified at Conciaro in January and keeps in the plant's store, so it books the truck from the plant to the factory: 4,800 slats on 24 heat-treated pallets, about 21.0 t, a quarter's supply for 960 benches, which the plant despatches for the sawmill. The slats remain the sawmill's until it delivers them. On the morning of the delivery it releases them from inward processing into free circulation in Urvania as their importer, pays Urvania's import VAT, assessed on the blanks as they were entered, and deducts it in its Urvanian return. It then sells them to Valdara from its stock in Urvania, a domestic sale in Urvania: registered for Urvania's VAT itself, it charges 20 % VAT, which Valdara deducts, and invoices in lume with the VAT also stated in ferra, at the parity of 100 ferra to the lume. The furniture maker buys timber at home; selling for manufacture, against the furniture maker's exemption certificate, the sawmill charges no sales tax and says why.

Receives

  • Import Customs Declaration from Marrowdale Sawmill: The sawmill, which owns the slats, releases them from inward processing into free circulation in Urvania before it sells them there; it pays Urvania's import VAT and deducts it under its Urvanian VAT number. See the example document.

    In it as CustomsParty.

    Also mentions: Valdara Industries as ConsigneeParty.

Modification: bench slats modified under contract (Bayvarra → Urvania)

From October to March, the southern summer, Marrowdale Sawmill's thermal modification kiln is full with its own decking and cladding, so it buys kiln slots for the park-bench slats from Tostara Wood Modification at Conciaro, east of Ferrano, under a season agreement that gives each slot a job number in advance. In January it orders one job for its season stock of park-bench slats, the standing product it sells to Valdara each quarter: the modification, planing, end sealing and bundling of 96 bundles. It sends its own kiln-dried ash blanks, 4,800 of 1,830 × 101 × 51 mm, across the border in two loads of its timber truck. The blanks remain the sawmill's and are only worked in Urvania, so they do not enter free circulation: the plant holds an authorisation for inward processing and enters them under it, and Urvania's import VAT waits until the sawmill releases the finished slats to sell them in Urvania. Bayvarra records the blanks as a permanent export for processing. The plant reports what it holds for the sawmill and when the second charge is ready, stores the bundles until the sawmill calls them off, and hands them to the carrier the sawmill books, against the sawmill's pick-up release. It invoices the processing in ferra, a service to a customer established abroad and so outside Urvania's VAT, and the sawmill pays within the discount period.

Receives

  • Import Customs Declaration from Tostara Wood Modification: The plant, holder of the authorisation, enters the blanks into inward processing: no import VAT while they are worked, for they are not released for free circulation. See the example document.

    In it as CustomsParty.

    Also mentions: Marrowdale Sawmill as ConsignorParty.

Gas and electricity in an unbundled market (Bayvarra's gas field → Urvania)

Urvania's energy market is unbundled: a power station produces electricity, a grid operator runs the networks and reads the meters, and a retail company buys the energy and bills the customers. The gas comes from an offshore field off Bayvarra's north coast, treated at the producer's plant on the shore and sent by pipeline across the border. The producer sells the month's gas to the power station and to the retail company for its gas customers; it declares the export in one declaration, with a consignment for each buyer, and each buyer declares its own import. The documents show the grid operator's metering with the correction a gas meter needs, the wholesale and network invoices, and a reminder across currencies when the power station pays late.

Receives
