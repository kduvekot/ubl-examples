## 2. Changes in this run

**Owner's decisions:** one bench design throughout the world: slats of thermally modified ash 1,800 x 95 x 45 mm and stainless fixings in every lot (the oak comparison of run 2 and the 'oak until May' story are dropped); two side frames, the 3 + 2 layout and the overall design kept; the frame material stays cast steel for now and is a parameter (`materials.frame_material`), because the owner is deciding between cast steel, ductile iron and aluminium.

**What the 45 mm slats changed in the design (revision B), and why:**

- **Slat seats and supports:** the seat-rail and back-rail flanges are the slat seats; their positions follow the slat thickness. To keep the seat surface at 450 mm with the 10 mm levelling gap (range 440–460 mm), the seat rail moves 7 mm down (normal to the seat) and the back rail 7 mm back (normal to the back). Flange widths, sections and the 1,600 mm slat span are unchanged.
- **Seat height:** 450 mm at the front edge, 440–460 mm, kept. With the run-2 frame it would have been 457 mm (447–467 mm).
- **Back:** surface, angle and slat positions unchanged; the top of the back stays at 780 mm. The back rail's top is shortened from 360 to 360 mm along the back so that the depth stays 620 mm (top of the back rail at 620 mm); it still carries the upper slat's bolts. Clearances around the armrest: 36 mm above the lower back slat, 41 mm below the upper one at its front face and 27 mm at its rear edge, all above the 25 mm finger-trap limit.
- **Bolts:** stack 45 + 8 + 1.6 + 6.8 mm plus at least 2.5 mm beyond the nut → **M8 x 65** carriage bolts (stainless A2-70; ISO 8677 length series from general knowledge). End distance 80 mm (10 d) and edge distance 47.5 mm (5.9 d) unchanged; with the brittle wood the square neck's seat stays pre-formed, and its 5 mm depth is a smaller part of a 45 mm slat.
- **Packaging:** bundle 1,810 x 295 x 100 mm; pallet of four 744 mm high (run 2: 716); still three layers by height in the trailer and both containers, 21 / 18 / 21 floor positions. Frame carton 786 x 676 x 98 mm (slightly shorter: shorter back rail).
- **Model:** the oak case is removed from the model, the drawings, the renders and the report; the frame material is selected in `parameters.json`.

## 4. The design and the reasons for it

### 4.1 Ergonomics and overall form

- **Seat height 450 mm** at the front edge, frames 10 mm above the plinth on the anchors' levelling nuts (0–20 mm gives 440–460 mm), grouted after levelling. Inclusive Mobility (2021, p. 102) asks 470–480 mm for most seats; the 2010 ADA Standards (903.5) allow 430–485 mm.
- **Armrests 200 mm above the seat** (Inclusive Mobility); seat width between the armrests 1,540 mm = 3 x 513 mm.
- **Seat tilt 4°, seat-to-back 104°** (back 18° from vertical), within the usual 0–5° and 100–110° (Pheasant and Haslegrave, *Bodyspace*, from general knowledge).
- **Seat depth 400 mm**: 329 mm of slat surface and a 71 mm open throat. **Back:** slats at 71–162 and 268–359 mm above the seat-back junction; top 331 mm above the seat front.

### 4.2 Side frame

- One symmetric casting for both sides (VI-C180), T-sections with the flange centred on the web, parting at the web mid-plane, no cores. Members: front post (35 mm, flange 30), armrest (30, pad 60), seat rail (35, flange 68), back rail (30, flange 68), rear leg (35, flange 30); feet 70 x 120 x 10 mm with one Ø14 anchor hole each. Walls 8 mm.
- Material for this run: **GE240+N (EN 10293, 1.0446)** (Rp0.2 ≥ 240 MPa). Hot-dip galvanised to EN ISO 1461 (castings ≥ 6 mm: at least 575 g/m²; 0.26 kg minimum, 0.32 kg typical per frame), designed to ISO 14713-2 (open sections); duplex powder coat 70 µm (0.05 kg).

### 4.3 Fixings

- Stainless A2-70 carriage bolts **M8 x 65** (ISO 8677), A2-70 nuts, A2 washers. Thermally modified timber is acidic (pH about 4) and the producers' handbook requires stainless A2 or acid-resistant A4 fasteners. **A4 is needed** where chlorides reach the bench, within roughly 1 km of the sea or on salted roads (EN 1993-1-4 Annex A approach, from general knowledge): in the world, on Lisavu's coast and in harbour towns.
- Stainless bolts on a galvanised frame form a bimetallic contact; acceptable in rural and urban air, with isolating washers at the coast (general knowledge).
- Holes pre-drilled (Ø9 in the slats, Ø10 in the flanges); end distance 80 mm = 10 d, edge distance 47.5 mm = 5.9 d (EN 1995-1-1 minima max(7d; 80 mm) and 3d, from general knowledge); square neck seat pre-formed (8.2 mm, 5 mm deep); torque 10 N m. Masses: bolt 30.0 g, washer 1.8 g, nut 5.2 g; 20 sets 0.74 kg, with the bag 0.76 kg.

## 8. Inconsistencies found

**Resolved since run 2:** the slats' lack of margin (run 2 items 15 and 16): with 45 mm the utilisation is 0.64, 0.74 in the 30 % case; and the oak benches already made (the owner's single design removes them from the world). Earlier: ash and beech not durable outdoors (run 1), the centre support (rejected).

**1. Length 1,800 mm 'over the armrests' with slats of 1,800 mm.** The bolts sit 80 mm from the slat ends (EN 1995-1-1); with two bolts either side of the web at ±20 mm the frame centre is 100 mm in, and the 60 mm armrest pads end 70 mm in from the slat ends: **1,660 mm** over the armrests.
*Options:* (a) 1,800 mm over the slats, 1,660 over the armrests; (b) wider armrest pads (+2.4 kg per frame); (c) longer slats. *Recommendation:* (a).

**2. Side frame 10.0 kg.** At 8 mm walls the cast-steel frame is **12.81 kg**; about 10 kg only at 6 mm (10.2 kg bare), thin for long steel members. Section 9 shows what other materials would weigh.
*Options:* (a) about 13 kg; (b) 6 mm walls; (c) another material (section 9). *Recommendation:* (a) for cast steel; the owner decides the material.

**3. Zinc 0.2 kg per frame, no coating mass.** EN ISO 1461 gives at least 0.26 kg on this frame, typically 0.32 kg; the powder coat adds 0.05 kg.
*Options:* (a) 0.3 + 0.05 kg; (b) keep 0.2 rounded. *Recommendation:* (a).

**4. Net 43.5 kg leaves out the zinc, and the parcels have weightless cartons.** D20: 2 x 10.0 + 5 x 4.5 + 1.0 = 43.5 kg without zinc; the 10.2 kg frame carton is frame plus zinc. A tray-and-lid carton weighs 1.1 kg.
*Options:* (a) net 48.8, gross 51.6 kg. *Recommendation:* (a).

**5. Frame carton 820 x 620 x 80 mm.** The frame is 730 x 620 x 70 mm; the carton 786 x 676 x 98 mm.
*Options:* (a) about 790 x 680 x 100 mm. *Recommendation:* (a).

**6. Pallet of four 1,850 x 650 x 750 mm.** A 676 mm carton does not fit a 650 mm pallet; on 700 mm the load is 744 mm high (close to the world's 750). Vehicle counts hold.
*Options:* (a) 1,850 x 700 x about 745 mm. *Recommendation:* (a).

**7. Pallet weights 178 / 200 kg and 4.3 t for 96 benches.** Model: 206.6 / 234.7 kg; 24 + 1 pallets 5.84 t.
*Options:* (a) adopt; (b) 'about 200 kg'. *Recommendation:* (a).

**8. Metal poured 34 kg per bench at about 60 %.** Castings 25.6 kg: 34 kg would be a 75 % yield. Model: 47.7 kg at 54 % (16 risers per frame), or 36.7 kg at 70 % with junction risers only.
*Options:* (a) 48 kg, 54 %; (b) 37 kg, 70 % with centre-line porosity. *Recommendation:* (a); net liquid steel about 28 kg.

**9. Steel in the product about 21 kg; consumption 34 kg.** Carbon steel 26.1 kg, stainless 0.74 kg; 34 kg is neither the poured metal nor the net consumption.
*Options:* (a) the separate figures of section 5. *Recommendation:* (a).

**10. Spare frame VI-C180: 9.5 / 10.2 kg (items.json).** Finished 13.44 kg, boxed 14.5 kg; one part for both sides.
*Options:* (a) 13.4 / 14.5 kg. *Recommendation:* (a).

**11. items.json 38.0 / 41.5 kg (known; D20 supersedes).** Model 48.8 / 51.6 kg.
*Options:* update once. *Recommendation:* as planned.

**12. Slat bundle 1,800 x 200 x 120 mm.** 200 x 120 holds five slats only with a hole; 1,800 mm leaves no end protection.
*Options:* (a) 3 + 2, 1,810 x 295 x 100 mm. *Recommendation:* (a).

**13. Seat height 450 mm against the UK's 470–480 mm.** Below Inclusive Mobility's range; inside the ADA's 430–485 mm.
*Options:* (a) keep; (b) 470 with an 800 mm back. *Recommendation:* (a).

**14. The world's slats: '1,800 x 95 x 38, hardwood, kiln-dried 12 %, 4.5 kg', oak or ash.** Every lot now has thermally modified ash 1,800 x 95 x 45 mm at about 5 %, 4.24 kg. The bench description ('five hardwood slats'), the slat item, the sawmill's delivery (O66, 21.6 t of slats) and decision O119 (oak now, ash from May, 'kiln-dried ash and oak weigh about the same') all change. The sawmill (Marrowdale) must also modify the wood at about 212 °C, or have it done: a step the world's story does not have yet.
*Options:* (a) rewrite the slat item, the sawmill delivery and O119 for thermally modified ash 45 mm; (b) keep the old texts (contradicts the bench). *Recommendation:* (a).

**15. Thermally modified ash is not strength-graded, and Eurocode 5 does not cover it.** The producers say it is not strength-graded and 'must not be used for load-bearing structures'. The model's design value is an assumption; even with a 30 % loss the 45 mm slat is at 0.74. It is more brittle (impact strength about −25 %), which matters for misuse more than for the static test.
*Options:* (a) grade LS13 before modification, a type test by UR-SF-0450 and yearly samples; (b) a test per batch. *Recommendation:* (a).

**16. The carriage bolt's square neck in brittle wood.** A square neck pulled into a round hole cuts its own seat and can split wood whose splitting strength is 30–40 % lower.
*Options:* (a) pre-formed seat and 10 N m; (b) hexagon bolts on top. *Recommendation:* (a).

**17. Stainless bolts on galvanised frames, A2 at the coast.** A2 pits in chloride-laden air (Lisavu, harbour towns); stainless and zinc form a bimetallic contact.
*Options:* (a) A4 at coastal sites and on salted roads, isolating washers at the coast; (b) A4 for every bench. *Recommendation:* (b) for one bolt set; owner question Q2.

**18. Maintenance does not mention the wood.** The wood greys under UV; the producers recommend a surface treatment outdoors.
*Options:* (a) oil yearly; (b) accept silver-grey and say so. *Recommendation:* (b), with an end-grain sealer at the factory.

**19. Cast steel for a bench frame; handling by a shopper.** Cast bench ends are usually iron or aluminium (section 9). ISO 11228-1's 25 kg is for the adult working population; for the general domestic population it gives 10 kg, below the parcels of 15, 15 and 22 kg.
*Options:* (a) keep and say shoppers' parcels are carried to the car or delivered; (b) another frame material (section 9). *Recommendation:* (a), pending the owner's material decision.

## 9. Frame material: cast steel, ductile iron or cast aluminium (for this geometry)

The owner is deciding the frame material. This compares the three for the present frame geometry, without choosing. Masses are computed from the solid model at each wall thickness (with the 2 % draft allowance); stresses are the model's worst case scaled by the seat rail's section modulus; deflections scaled by stiffness. `materials.frame_material` in `parameters.json` switches the whole model to another material (its castings, risers and gating would then need rework, see the yield row).

| Material | Wall | Frame mass (kg) | Zinc (kg) | σ max (MPa) | 1.5 σ / (Rp0.2/γM) | max. deflection (mm) |

**Assessment (not a choice):** for this geometry all three carry the load with margin. Ductile iron gives the most ordinary bench end: about 9 kg per frame at 6 mm, a better casting yield, galvanising possible with care. Cast aluminium makes the lightest bench (frames of about 4.5 kg; the bench about 31 kg net with the same slats), with no zinc but a brittle alloy at 2 % elongation and a galvanic contact to the stainless bolts. Cast steel is the strongest and toughest but the heaviest and hardest to cast thin, and it is the least usual. The figures for minimum walls and yields of iron and aluminium are judgements.

## 10. Questions for the owner

- **Q1 – Frame material (section 9).** Cast steel, ductile iron or cast aluminium; the model switches with one parameter.
- **Q2 – A2 or A4 (item 17).** Recommendation: A4 for every bench.
- **Q3 – Slat story (item 14).** Recommendation: rewrite the slat item, the sawmill delivery (O66) and O119 for thermally modified ash 45 mm, and give Marrowdale a modification kiln or a subcontractor.
- **Q4 – Steel figure for O25 (items 8, 9).** Recommendation: net liquid steel about 28 kg, stainless 0.74 kg separately.
- **Q5 – Proof of the modified slats (item 15).** Recommendation: a type test by UR-SF-0450 and yearly samples.
