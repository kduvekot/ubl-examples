## 2. Changes in this run

**Owner's decisions applied:** slats of thermally modified European ash (modified at about 212 °C) from May 2026 as the main case, oak heartwood (EN 338 D30) for the current delivery as the comparison; fixings in stainless steel A2, all holes pre-drilled; no centre leg; design, layout and drawings kept. **What the material change forced in the design:** nothing in the frame or the slat layout. ## 4. The design and the reasons for it

### 4.1 Ergonomics and overall form (unchanged)

- **Seat height 450 mm** at the front edge, frames 10 mm above the plinth on the anchors' levelling nuts (0 to 20 mm gives 440 to 460 mm), grouted after levelling. Inclusive Mobility (2021, p. 102) asks 470–480 mm for most seats; the 2010 ADA Standards (903.5) allow 430–485 mm.
- **Armrests 200 mm above the seat** (Inclusive Mobility); seat width between the armrests 1,540 mm = 3 x 513 mm (at least 500 per person).
- **Seat tilt 4°, seat-to-back 104°** (back 18° from vertical), within the usual 0–5° and 100–110° of seating handbooks (Pheasant and Haslegrave, *Bodyspace*, from general knowledge).
- **Seat depth 400 mm**: three slats and two 22 mm gaps give 329 mm of slat surface and a 71 mm open throat. **Back:** two slats at 71–162 and 268–359 mm above the seat-back junction; top 331 mm above the seat front.
- **Depth 620 mm**: rear feet at 620 mm, top of the back rail at 616 mm. Gaps around the armrest 36 and 41 mm, outside the 8–25 mm finger-trap range (EN 1176-1 as a reference, from general knowledge).

### 4.2 Side frame (unchanged)

- One symmetric casting for both sides (VI-C180), T-sections with the flange centred on the web, parting at the web mid-plane, no cores. Members: front post (35 mm, flange 30), armrest (30, pad 60), seat rail (35, flange 68), back rail (30, flange 68), rear leg (35, flange 30); two feet 70 x 120 x 10 mm with one Ø14 anchor hole each. Walls 8 mm (SFSA: 6 mm minimum for sand-cast steel, plus a margin for flow length); at 6 mm the frame would weigh 10.2 kg, at 8 mm 12.8 kg (bare).
- GE240+N to EN 10293 (Rp0.2 ≥ 240 MPa). Hot-dip galvanised to EN ISO 1461 (castings ≥ 6 mm: at least 575 g/m², 0.26 kg; typical 0.32 kg per frame), designed to ISO 14713-2 (open sections, hung from the anchor holes); duplex powder coat 70 µm (0.05 kg). Melt number cast in per casting day; serial number hard-stamped after fettling.

### 4.3 Fixings: stainless steel (changed)

- **Why stainless:** thermally modified timber is more acidic than untreated wood (pH about 4 against 4.5–5 for the untreated wood, per the producers' handbook, which also states that fasteners must be stainless A2 or acid-resistant A4, indoors and outdoors). Oak is acidic too and rich in tannins, which corrode zinc and stain black around plain or galvanised steel (general knowledge). Stainless therefore serves both woods, and the bolt set stays one item.
- **Grade:** A2-70 bolts and nuts, A2 washers (EN 1.4301). **A4 (EN 1.4401) is needed** where chlorides reach the bench: within roughly 1 km of the sea or where de-icing salt is used (the corrosion-resistance approach of EN 1993-1-4 Annex A; quoted from general knowledge). In the world that means benches on the island of Lisavu's coast and in harbour towns such as Lumeport, and every bench on roads that are salted (owner question Q2).
- **Stainless bolts on a galvanised frame:** a bimetallic contact. The zinc is the large anode and the stainless bolt the small cathode, an arrangement that is normally acceptable in rural and urban air; in coastal air the zinc around the hole corrodes faster. Recommendation: a nylon or EPDM isolating washer between the stainless washer and the coated flange at coastal sites (general knowledge; not checked against a standard).
- **Holes and distances:** all holes are pre-drilled, Ø9 in the slats and Ø10 in the flanges. End distance 80 mm = 10 d from the slat end and edge distance 47.5 mm = 5.9 d (bolts on the centre line of the width), against EN 1995-1-1's minima of max(7d; 80 mm) and 3d (from general knowledge). Bolts loaded across the grain are not the issue (the seat load goes through bearing on the flange); splitting is, and thermally modified wood splits 30–40 % more easily (handbook). The distances are kept; what changes is that the bolt's square neck must not be wedged into the wood: in thermally modified ash its seat is pre-formed, and the torque is limited.
- **Masses:** bolt 28.2 g, washer 1.8 g, nut 5.2 g (stainless, 7,930 kg/m³, the bolt's thread taken as 0.93 of a full shank); 20 sets 0.70 kg, with the bag 0.72 kg.

## 7. Inconsistencies found

**Resolved by the owner's decisions since run 1:** run 1's item 14 (ash and beech not durable outdoors: the ash is now thermally modified, durability class 2 by the producers' data, and beech is no longer named) and owner question Q2 of run 1 (a centre support: rejected; the slats stay on two frames, item 16 below records what that means). Run 1's item 15 (moisture) is restated for both woods (item 14).

**1. Length 1,800 mm 'over the armrests' with slats of 1,800 mm.** The bolts sit 80 mm from the slat ends (EN 1995-1-1 end distance max(7d; 80 mm)); with the two bolts either side of the web at ±20 mm the frame centre is 100 mm from each end, and the 60 mm armrest pads end 70 mm in from the slat ends: 1,800 − 2 x 70 = **1,660 mm**. Moving the bolts nearer the end grain is less acceptable than before, because the modified ash splits more easily.
*Options:* (a) 1,800 mm over the slats, 1,660 mm over the armrests; (b) armrest pads widened outward (+2.4 kg per frame); (c) longer slats (excluded). *Recommendation:* (a).

**2. Side frame 10.0 kg.** At castable 8 mm walls the frame is **12.80 kg** cast; about 10 kg only at 6 mm walls (10.2 kg bare), too thin for 0.5 m members of steel fed from one end. Strength does not set the walls.
*Options:* (a) about 13.1 kg cast, 13.4 kg finished; (b) 6 mm walls with misrun risk; (c) ductile iron. *Recommendation:* (a).

**3. Zinc 0.2 kg per frame, no coating mass.** EN ISO 1461 gives at least 575 g/m² on castings; on 0.446 m² that is 0.26 kg, typically 0.32 kg; the powder coat adds 0.05 kg.
*Options:* (a) 0.3 + 0.05 kg per frame; (b) keep 0.2 as rounded. *Recommendation:* (a).

**4. Net 43.5 kg leaves out the zinc, and the parcels have weightless cartons.** D20: 2 x 10.0 + 5 x 4.5 + 1.0 = 43.5 kg net without zinc; the 10.2 kg frame carton is frame plus zinc, the carton weighing nothing. A tray-and-lid carton weighs 1.1 kg.
*Options:* (a) net 45.5 kg (oak 49.8), gross 48.3 kg (oak 52.7); (b) D20 parts plus zinc and packaging. *Recommendation:* (a).

**5. Frame carton 820 x 620 x 80 mm.** The frame itself is 742 x 620 x 70 mm; with clearance the carton is 798 x 676 x 98 mm.
*Options:* (a) about 800 x 680 x 100 mm. *Recommendation:* (a).

*Options:* (a) keep 450; (b) 470 with an 800 mm back. *Recommendation:* (a).

**14. Slats 'kiln-dried to 12 % (10–14 %)' and 4.5 kg each, for both deliveries (new).** The modified ash leaves the process at about 4–7 % and weighs 554 kg/m³: a slat of **3.58 kg**, not 4.5 kg, and not 'kiln-dried to 12 %'. Bench net falls by 4.4 kg from May; the slat bundle by 4.4 kg. The sawmill's truck of 4,800 slats (O66) weighs 17.2 t in modified ash against 21.4 t in oak (the decision text says 21.6 t at 4.5 kg). The decision of O119 says 'kiln-dried ash and oak weigh about the same (D20: about 690)': true for untreated ash, not for the modified ash. Outdoors the oak settles at about 15–20 % (about 5 % heavier, 2–3 mm wider); the modified ash at about 8–12 % (about 5 % heavier than delivered, about 1 mm wider).
*Options:* (a) the item description says 'five hardwood slats' without a moisture figure, and the weights follow the delivery (lot) of each document; (b) one average figure for both woods (wrong for both). *Recommendation:* (a); weights stated 'as delivered'.

**15. Thermally modified ash is not strength-graded, and Eurocode 5 does not cover it (new).** The producers' handbook states that thermally modified products are not available as strength-graded timber and 'must not be used for load-bearing structures'. A bench slat is a load-bearing furniture part, not a building structure, but its design value is still unknown. The model grades the ash before modification (LS13, D40) and reduces it by the clear-wood ratio: utilisation 0.88 under the 450 kg test. With a 30 % loss instead of 19 % it would be 1.02. The wood is also more brittle (impact strength about 25 % lower), which matters for misuse (people standing or jumping on the seat) more than for the static test. A batch from Upland State is not an EN 1912 assignment either: EN 1912 covers ash from Germany, Austria and Switzerland, and the world's ash would need its own assignment.
*Options:* (a) grade LS13 before modification, prove each modification batch by a bending test on sample slats (for example the 450 kg test on a bench, or EN 408 on slats) and state it in the datasheet; (b) a stiffer section (excluded: slats fixed); (c) back to oak (excluded by the owner). *Recommendation:* (a); the world's test standard UR-SF-0450 can carry the proof.

**16. Two frames and no centre leg: the slats are at their limit (restated).** Oak: σ = 13.4 MPa, utilisation 0.95 (EN 581-2 centre seat 0.98), sag 17 mm under the test and 9 mm under three adults. Thermally modified ash: utilisation 0.88 (0.91), sag 14 and 8 mm, better on paper only because it starts from D40.
*Options:* (a) accept and state the sag in the datasheet; (b) a centre support (rejected by the owner). *Recommendation:* (a).

**17. The carriage bolt's square neck in brittle wood (new).** A cup-square-neck bolt is normally pulled into a round hole, its neck cutting its own square seat; in wood whose splitting strength is 30–40 % lower this can crack the slat at the bolt, 80 mm from the end.
*Options:* (a) pre-form the square seat (8.2 mm, 5 mm deep) and limit the torque to 10 N m in modified ash; (b) hexagon bolts with large washers on top (a protruding head on the seat). *Recommendation:* (a); shown on drawings 003 and 004.

**18. Stainless bolts on galvanised frames, and A2 at the coast (new).** A2 resists the acid wood but pits in chloride-laden air; part of the world's benches stand on Lisavu and in harbour towns. The stainless bolt also forms a bimetallic contact with the zinc of the frame.
*Options:* (a) A4 for coastal sites and salted roads, A2 elsewhere, with isolating washers at the coast; (b) A4 for every bench (one bolt set, slightly dearer). *Recommendation:* (b) for simplicity of stock, or (a) if the owner wants two variants; owner question Q2.

**19. Maintenance 'tighten the slat bolts and touch up the coating once a year' (new).** Both woods grey under UV; the producers recommend a surface treatment for thermally modified timber exposed to the weather. Oak also checks at the ends without a finish. The yearly maintenance does not mention the wood.
*Options:* (a) add 'oil the slats' to the yearly maintenance; (b) accept greying (silver-grey slats are common on park benches) and say so. *Recommendation:* (b), with an end-grain sealer at the factory.

**20. Cast steel for a bench frame; handling by a shopper.** Cast bench ends are usually iron or aluminium; steel needs many risers (item 8). ISO 11228-1's 25 kg is for the adult working population; for the general domestic population it gives 10 kg, below the parcels of 15, 15 and 18 kg. The 'two thirds of combined capacity' rule for two people was not found in the accessible parts of ISO 11228-1.
*Options:* (a) keep; parcels for shoppers carried to the car or delivered; (b) ductile iron. *Recommendation:* (a).

Figures that **hold**: depth 620, height 780, seat height 450 (440–460); four anchors; 20 bolts; slat 1,800 x 95 x 38; the load test UR-SF-0450 (frame comfortably; slats just, see items 15 and 16); repairability; 21 / 18 floor positions and two layers; the parcels within the handling limits; the four-bench pallet by fork-lift, stacking two or three high (by height).

## 8. Questions for the owner

- **Q1 – Proof of the modified slats (item 15).** Options: a bending proof test per modification batch; accept the model's assumed design value; a grading assignment for the world's ash. Recommendation: the proof test, written into the datasheet and the sawmill's delivery.
- **Q2 – A2 or A4 (item 18).** Recommendation: A4 for every bench (one bolt set; the world sells the same bench inland and on the island).
- **Q3 – Weights per delivery (item 14).** Recommendation: documents for lots made from May use the modified-ash figures, earlier lots the oak figures.
- **Q4 – Steel figure for O25 (items 8, 9).** Recommendation: net liquid steel about 28 kg, plus 0.70 kg of stainless steel as a separate material.

## 9. Limits of the model

- The design values of the thermally modified ash are an assumption (item 15); everything else about the slats follows from them.
- Frame: plane-frame model, in-plane bending of T-sections; casting rules of thumb; draft as a mass allowance.
- Standards read in previews or summaries only: EN 581-2, EN 10293, EN 338, EN 350, EN 1912, ISO 14713-2, ISO 11228-1. From general knowledge: EN 1995-1-1 (spacings, k_mod, k_h, γM), EN 1990, EN 1993-1-4 (A2/A4 selection), EN 1176-1, the EN 338:2016 value E0,mean = 13,000 MPa for D40, fastener dimensions, FEFCO 0320, ISPM 15, ISO 668, *Bodyspace*.
- The handbook's strength and density data for modified ash are for small clear specimens (20 x 20 x 360 mm); graded slats with knots may lose more.
