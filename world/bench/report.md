## 2. Changes in this run

**Owner's decisions (run 3):** slats of thermally modified ash 1,800 x 95 x **45 mm** from May 2026; the benches already made keep oak 1,800 x 95 x 38 mm (comparison case); two side frames, the 3 + 2 layout and the overall design kept. Earlier decisions stay: stainless fixings, no centre leg, the name 'thermally modified ash' only.

**What the thicker slats changed in the design (frame revision B), and why:**

- **Slat seats:** the seat-rail and back-rail flanges are the slat seats; their positions follow the slat thickness. To keep the seat surface at 450 mm at the front edge with the levelling gap of 10 mm (so that the range 440–460 mm stays), the seat rail moves 7 mm down (normal to the seat) and the back rail 7 mm back (normal to the back). Flange widths (68 mm), sections and the slat span (1,600 mm) are unchanged.
- **Seat height:** kept at 450 mm, 440–460 mm. With the revision-A frame the seat would be at 457 mm and the range 447–467 mm.
- **Back:** the back surface, its angle and the slat positions are unchanged, so the top of the back stays at 780 mm. Because the back rail moved back, its top is shortened from 360 to 360 mm along the back to keep the depth at 620 mm (top of the back rail at 620 mm); it still carries the upper slat's bolts. The armrest still passes between the back slats: 36 mm above the lower slat; below the upper slat 41 mm at its front face and 27 mm at its rear edge, above the 25 mm finger-trap limit.
- **Bolts:** stack 45 + 8 + 1.6 + 6.8 mm plus at least 2.5 mm beyond the nut → carriage bolt **M8 x 65** (stainless A2-70) for revision B; M8 x 60 stays on revision A. End distance 80 mm (10 d) and edge distance 47.5 mm (5.9 d) are unchanged; they meet EN 1995-1-1's minima with margin and suit the more brittle wood. The thicker slat helps: the neck's 5 mm square seat is a smaller part of 45 mm than of 38 mm.
- **Packaging:** the 3 + 2 bundle grows to 1,810 x 295 x 100 mm; the pallet of four to 1,850 x 700 x 744 mm (run 2: 716); still three layers by height in the trailer and both containers, 21 / 18 / 21 floor positions. The frame carton is slightly shorter (786 mm) because the back rail is shorter.
- **Drawings, BOM, STEP and GLB** show revision B (45 mm slats, M8 x 65); the BOM lists the revision-A slat and bolt too. The oak render shows the revision-A bench.

## 4. The design and the reasons for it

### 4.1 Ergonomics and overall form (unchanged)

- **Seat height 450 mm** at the front edge, frames 10 mm above the plinth on the anchors' levelling nuts (0 to 20 mm gives 440 to 460 mm), grouted after levelling. Inclusive Mobility (2021, p. 102) asks 470–480 mm for most seats; the 2010 ADA Standards (903.5) allow 430–485 mm.
- **Armrests 200 mm above the seat** (Inclusive Mobility); seat width between the armrests 1,540 mm = 3 x 513 mm (at least 500 per person).
- **Seat tilt 4°, seat-to-back 104°** (back 18° from vertical), within the usual 0–5° and 100–110° of seating handbooks (Pheasant and Haslegrave, *Bodyspace*, from general knowledge).
- **Seat depth 400 mm**: three slats and two 22 mm gaps give 329 mm of slat surface and a 71 mm open throat. **Back:** two slats at 71–162 and 268–359 mm above the seat-back junction; top 331 mm above the seat front.
- **Depth 620 mm**: rear feet at 620 mm, top of the back rail at 620 mm. Gaps around the armrest 36 and 41 mm, outside the 8–25 mm finger-trap range (EN 1176-1 as a reference, from general knowledge).

### 4.2 Side frame (revision B: rails moved for 45 mm slats, back rail shortened)

- One symmetric casting for both sides (VI-C180), T-sections with the flange centred on the web, parting at the web mid-plane, no cores. Members: front post (35 mm, flange 30), armrest (30, pad 60), seat rail (35, flange 68), back rail (30, flange 68), rear leg (35, flange 30); two feet 70 x 120 x 10 mm with one Ø14 anchor hole each. Walls 8 mm (SFSA: 6 mm minimum for sand-cast steel, plus a margin for flow length); at 6 mm the frame would weigh 10.2 kg, at 8 mm 12.8 kg (bare).
- GE240+N to EN 10293 (Rp0.2 ≥ 240 MPa). Hot-dip galvanised to EN ISO 1461 (castings ≥ 6 mm: at least 575 g/m², 0.26 kg; typical 0.32 kg per frame), designed to ISO 14713-2 (open sections, hung from the anchor holes); duplex powder coat 70 µm (0.05 kg). Melt number cast in per casting day; serial number hard-stamped after fettling.

### 4.3 Fixings: stainless steel

- **Why stainless:** thermally modified timber is more acidic than untreated wood (pH about 4 against 4.5–5 for the untreated wood, per the producers' handbook, which also states that fasteners must be stainless A2 or acid-resistant A4, indoors and outdoors). Oak is acidic too and rich in tannins, which corrode zinc and stain black around plain or galvanised steel (general knowledge). Stainless therefore serves both woods. Lengths: M8 x 65 for 45 mm slats, M8 x 60 for 38 mm (ISO 8677 length series from general knowledge).
- **Grade:** A2-70 bolts and nuts, A2 washers (EN 1.4301). **A4 (EN 1.4401) is needed** where chlorides reach the bench: within roughly 1 km of the sea or where de-icing salt is used (the corrosion-resistance approach of EN 1993-1-4 Annex A; quoted from general knowledge). In the world that means benches on the island of Lisavu's coast and in harbour towns such as Lumeport, and every bench on roads that are salted (owner question Q2).
- **Stainless bolts on a galvanised frame:** a bimetallic contact. The zinc is the large anode and the stainless bolt the small cathode, an arrangement that is normally acceptable in rural and urban air; in coastal air the zinc around the hole corrodes faster. Recommendation: a nylon or EPDM isolating washer between the stainless washer and the coated flange at coastal sites (general knowledge; not checked against a standard).
- **Holes and distances:** all holes are pre-drilled, Ø9 in the slats and Ø10 in the flanges. End distance 80 mm = 10 d from the slat end and edge distance 47.5 mm = 5.9 d (bolts on the centre line of the width), against EN 1995-1-1's minima of max(7d; 80 mm) and 3d (from general knowledge). Bolts loaded across the grain are not the issue (the seat load goes through bearing on the flange); splitting is, and thermally modified wood splits 30–40 % more easily (handbook). The distances are kept; what changes is that the bolt's square neck must not be wedged into the wood: in thermally modified ash its seat is pre-formed, and the torque is limited.
- **Masses:** bolt 30.0 g, washer 1.8 g, nut 5.2 g (stainless, 7,930 kg/m³, the bolt's thread taken as 0.93 of a full shank); 20 sets 0.74 kg, with the bag 0.76 kg.

## 7. Inconsistencies found

**Resolved since run 1:** run 1's item 14 (ash and beech not durable outdoors; the ash is now thermally modified) and the centre-support question (rejected). **Resolved by run 3's thicker slats, for benches from May:** run 2's item 16 (slats at their limit) and most of run 2's item 15 (the slat's margin resting on an assumed design value): with 45 mm the utilisation is 0.64, and 0.74 even if the modification costs 30 %. Both remain for the oak benches already made (item 16).

**1. Length 1,800 mm 'over the armrests' with slats of 1,800 mm.** The bolts sit 80 mm from the slat ends (EN 1995-1-1 end distance max(7d; 80 mm)); with the two bolts either side of the web at ±20 mm the frame centre is 100 mm from each end, and the 60 mm armrest pads end 70 mm in from the slat ends: 1,800 − 2 x 70 = **1,660 mm**. Moving the bolts nearer the end grain is less acceptable than before, because the modified ash splits more easily.
*Options:* (a) 1,800 mm over the slats, 1,660 mm over the armrests; (b) armrest pads widened outward (+2.4 kg per frame); (c) longer slats (excluded). *Recommendation:* (a).

**2. Side frame 10.0 kg.** At castable 8 mm walls the frame is **12.81 kg** cast; about 10 kg only at 6 mm walls (10.2 kg bare), too thin for 0.5 m members of steel fed from one end. Strength does not set the walls.
*Options:* (a) about 13.1 kg cast, 13.4 kg finished; (b) 6 mm walls with misrun risk; (c) ductile iron. *Recommendation:* (a).

**3. Zinc 0.2 kg per frame, no coating mass.** EN ISO 1461 gives at least 575 g/m² on castings; on 0.446 m² that is 0.26 kg, typically 0.32 kg; the powder coat adds 0.05 kg.
*Options:* (a) 0.3 + 0.05 kg per frame; (b) keep 0.2 as rounded. *Recommendation:* (a).

**4. Net 43.5 kg leaves out the zinc, and the parcels have weightless cartons.** D20: 2 x 10.0 + 5 x 4.5 + 1.0 = 43.5 kg net without zinc; the 10.2 kg frame carton is frame plus zinc, the carton weighing nothing. A tray-and-lid carton weighs 1.1 kg.
*Options:* (a) net 48.8 kg (oak 49.8), gross 51.6 kg (oak 52.7); (b) D20 parts plus zinc and packaging. *Recommendation:* (a).

**5. Frame carton 820 x 620 x 80 mm.** The frame itself is 730 x 620 x 70 mm; with clearance the carton is 786 x 676 x 98 mm.
*Options:* (a) about 800 x 680 x 100 mm. *Recommendation:* (a).

**6. Pallet of four 1,850 x 650 x 750 mm.** A 676 mm carton does not fit a 650 mm pallet; on a 700 mm pallet the load is 744 mm high. The vehicle counts hold.
*Options:* (a) 1,850 x 700 x about 720 mm; (b) cartons on edge (not stackable). *Recommendation:* (a).

**7. Pallet weights 178 / 200 kg and 4.3 t for 96 benches.** Thermally modified ash: 206.6 / 234.7 kg, 5.84 t for 24 + 1 pallets; oak: 210.7 / 238.9 kg, 5.94 t.
*Options:* (a) adopt per delivery; (b) keep 'about 200 kg'. *Recommendation:* (a).

**8. Metal poured 34 kg per bench at about 60 %.** The castings weigh 25.6 kg; 34 kg would be a 75 % yield. Model: 47.7 kg at 54 % (16 risers per frame by the SFSA rule), or 36.7 kg at 70 % with junction risers only.
*Options:* (a) 49 kg, 52 %; (b) 37 kg, 70 % with centre-line porosity; (c) 34 kg (impossible). *Recommendation:* (a); net liquid steel about 28 kg per bench.

**9. Steel in the product about 21 kg; steel consumption 34 kg.** Carbon steel in the product 26.1 kg (castings); the fixings are now 0.74 kg of stainless steel, a separate material. 34 kg is neither the poured metal (47.7 kg) nor the net consumption (about 28 kg).
*Options:* (a) state carbon steel, stainless steel and liquid steel separately (table 5.1). *Recommendation:* (a), for O25.

**10. Spare frame VI-C180: 9.5 / 10.2 kg (items.json).** Finished frame 13.44 kg, boxed 14.5 kg; one part for both sides thanks to the symmetric T-sections.
*Options:* (a) 13.4 / 14.5 kg. *Recommendation:* (a).

**11. items.json 38.0 / 41.5 kg (known; D20 supersedes).** Neither matches the model: 48.8 / 51.6 kg with thermally modified ash, 49.8 / 52.7 kg with oak.
*Options:* update once, with the figures of the delivery each document's lot comes from. *Recommendation:* as planned.

**12. Slat bundle 1,800 x 200 x 120 mm.** With end protection a bundle is about 1,810 mm long; 200 x 120 holds five slats only as 2 x 3 with a hole.
*Options:* (a) 3 + 2 pattern, 1,810 x 295 x 100 mm. *Recommendation:* (a).

**13. Seat height 450 mm against the UK's 470–480 mm.** Below Inclusive Mobility's range for most seats; inside the ADA's 430–485 mm.
*Options:* (a) keep 450; (b) 470 with an 800 mm back. *Recommendation:* (a).

**14. Slats '1,800 x 95 x 38, kiln-dried to 12 % (10–14 %), 4.5 kg' for both deliveries.** From May the slat is 1,800 x 95 x 45 mm, at about 4–7 % moisture and 554 kg/m³: **4.24 kg**, not 4.5 kg, not 38 mm and not 'kiln-dried to 12 %'. Bench net from May 48.8 kg against 49.8 kg; the slat bundle 21.8 against 22.9 kg. The sawmill's truck (O66) carries 21.0 t with pallets (20.3 t of slats; the decision text says 21.6 t of slats at 4.5 kg). The world's description 'fixed by the sawmill' now has two sizes. The decision of O119 says 'kiln-dried ash and oak weigh about the same (D20: about 690)': true for untreated ash, not for the modified ash. Outdoors the oak settles at about 15–20 % (about 5 % heavier, 2–3 mm wider); the modified ash at about 8–12 % (about 5 % heavier than delivered, about 1 mm wider).
*Options:* (a) the item description says 'five hardwood slats' without size or moisture, and size and weights follow the delivery (lot) of each document; (b) one average figure for both (wrong for both). *Recommendation:* (a); weights stated 'as delivered'.

**15. Thermally modified ash is not strength-graded, and Eurocode 5 does not cover it (margin now restored).** The producers' handbook states that thermally modified products are not available as strength-graded timber and 'must not be used for load-bearing structures'. A bench slat is a load-bearing furniture part, not a building structure, but its design value is still unknown. The model grades the ash before modification (LS13, D40) and reduces it by the clear-wood ratio: with 45 mm slats the utilisation is 0.64 under the 450 kg test, and 0.74 with a 30 % loss instead of 19 % (run 2 with 38 mm: 0.88 and 1.02). The wood is also more brittle (impact strength about 25 % lower), which matters for misuse (people standing or jumping on the seat) more than for the static test. A batch from Upland State is not an EN 1912 assignment either: EN 1912 covers ash from Germany, Austria and Switzerland, and the world's ash would need its own assignment.
*Options:* (a) grade LS13 before modification and prove the type once by the 450 kg test (UR-SF-0450) on a bench, then sample tests per year; (b) a proof test per modification batch (as recommended in run 2, now more than the margin needs). *Recommendation:* (a).

**16. The oak benches already made: slats at their limit (resolved for benches from May).** Oak 38 mm: σ = 13.4 MPa, utilisation 0.95 (EN 581-2 centre seat 0.98), sag 17 mm under the test and 9 mm under three adults. Modified ash 45 mm: 0.64 (0.66), sag 8.7 and 4.8 mm.
*Options:* (a) accept for the benches already made (they pass the test); (b) replace their slats with 45 mm slats on revision-B frames (not possible on revision-A frames without changing the seat height by 7 mm). *Recommendation:* (a).

**17. Frame revision B and the spare part VI-C180 (new).** Revision B (45 mm slats) has its seat and back rails 7 mm lower and further back than revision A. 'The spare part is one frame' no longer holds for both kinds of bench. A revision-B frame fitted to an old bench with 38 mm slats puts that side's slats 7 mm low; raising that side by 7 mm on the levelling nuts (gap 10 → 17 mm, within 0–20) brings the slats level again; the back slats then sit 7 mm further back on that side, which a 1,800 mm slat absorbs.
*Options:* (a) one spare part, revision B, with a fitting note for old benches (raise 7 mm on the levelling nuts); (b) two spare parts, VI-C180 rev. A and rev. B; (c) keep revision A for both (seat 457 mm, range 447–467, outside the stated 440–460). *Recommendation:* (a); owner question Q5.

**18. Two bolt lengths and two slat items (new).** Revision B needs M8 x 65, revision A M8 x 60: the bolt set (in the second frame carton) differs by bench. A single M8 x 65 set would leave 10.6 mm of thread beyond the nut on old benches (snag under the seat). The slat item and the sawmill's delivery also get a second size.
*Options:* (a) bolt sets per revision (M8 x 65 from May; M8 x 60 as a spare set for old benches); (b) one set of M8 x 65 with dome nuts on old benches. *Recommendation:* (a); owner question Q6.

**19. The carriage bolt's square neck in brittle wood (since run 2).** A cup-square-neck bolt is normally pulled into a round hole, its neck cutting its own square seat; in wood whose splitting strength is 30–40 % lower this can crack the slat at the bolt, 80 mm from the end.
*Options:* (a) pre-form the square seat (8.2 mm, 5 mm deep) and limit the torque to 10 N m in modified ash; (b) hexagon bolts with large washers on top (a protruding head on the seat). *Recommendation:* (a); shown on drawings 003 and 004.

**20. Stainless bolts on galvanised frames, and A2 at the coast (since run 2).** A2 resists the acid wood but pits in chloride-laden air; part of the world's benches stand on Lisavu and in harbour towns. The stainless bolt also forms a bimetallic contact with the zinc of the frame.
*Options:* (a) A4 for coastal sites and salted roads, A2 elsewhere, with isolating washers at the coast; (b) A4 for every bench (one bolt set, slightly dearer). *Recommendation:* (b), or (a) if the owner wants two variants; owner question Q2.

**21. Maintenance 'tighten the slat bolts and touch up the coating once a year' (since run 2).** Both woods grey under UV; the producers recommend a surface treatment for thermally modified timber exposed to the weather. Oak also checks at the ends without a finish. The yearly maintenance does not mention the wood.
*Options:* (a) add 'oil the slats' to the yearly maintenance; (b) accept greying (silver-grey slats are common on park benches) and say so. *Recommendation:* (b), with an end-grain sealer at the factory.

**22. Cast steel for a bench frame; handling by a shopper.** Cast bench ends are usually iron or aluminium; steel needs many risers (item 8). ISO 11228-1's 25 kg is for the adult working population; for the general domestic population it gives 10 kg, below the parcels of 15, 15 and 22 kg. The 'two thirds of combined capacity' rule for two people was not found in the accessible parts of ISO 11228-1.
*Options:* (a) keep; parcels for shoppers carried to the car or delivered; (b) ductile iron. *Recommendation:* (a).

Figures that **hold**: depth 620, height 780, seat height 450 (440–460) on both revisions; four anchors; 20 bolts; the load test UR-SF-0450 (frame comfortably; 45 mm slats with margin, 38 mm oak slats just); repairability; 21 / 18 floor positions and two layers; the parcels within the handling limits; the four-bench pallet by fork-lift, stacking two or three high (by height).

- **Q2 – A2 or A4 (item 20).** Recommendation: A4 for every bench (one bolt set; the world sells the same bench inland and on the island).
- **Q3 – Weights per delivery (item 14).** Recommendation: documents for lots made from May use the modified-ash figures, earlier lots the oak figures.
- **Q4 – Steel figure for O25 (items 8, 9).** Recommendation: net liquid steel about 28 kg, plus 0.74 kg of stainless steel as a separate material.
- **Q5 – Spare frame (item 17).** Recommendation: one spare part, revision B, with the note to raise an old bench's side 7 mm on the levelling nuts.
- **Q6 – Bolt sets (item 18).** Recommendation: two bolt sets, M8 x 65 for benches from May and M8 x 60 for the old ones.

## 9. Limits of the model

- The design values of the thermally modified ash are an assumption (item 15); everything else about the slats follows from them.
- Frame: plane-frame model, in-plane bending of T-sections; casting rules of thumb; draft as a mass allowance.
- Standards read in previews or summaries only: EN 581-2, EN 10293, EN 338, EN 350, EN 1912, ISO 14713-2, ISO 11228-1. From general knowledge: EN 1995-1-1 (spacings, k_mod, k_h, γM), EN 1990, EN 1993-1-4 (A2/A4 selection), EN 1176-1, the EN 338:2016 value E0,mean = 13,000 MPa for D40, fastener dimensions, FEFCO 0320, ISPM 15, ISO 668, *Bodyspace*.
- The handbook's strength and density data for modified ash are for small clear specimens (20 x 20 x 360 mm); graded slats with knots may lose more.
