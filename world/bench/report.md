## 2. Changes in this run

**Owner's decision (run 4):** the side frames are ductile cast iron EN-GJS-400-15 (EN 1563), made by Valdara from Serravona ore and cast in its own works. Everything else as in run 3: thermally modified ash slats 1,800 x 95 x 45 mm, stainless fixings, the 3 + 2 layout, two frames, size and seat height.

**What changed in the design (revision C), and why:**

- **Wall thickness 7 mm** (was 8 mm in steel). Sources for sand-cast ductile iron disagree: foundry guides give 5–6 mm in green sand (Minncast; casting-china.org), 5–7 mm as practical minima (metal-castings.com), and Farrar recommends at least 0.300 in (7.6 mm); Bralla's *Design for Manufacturability Handbook* (via an NPTEL course) makes the minimum depend on length: 5 mm for sections up to 300 mm, 13 mm up to 1.2 m. The members here are 240–550 mm long between junctions, each fed from a gate: interpolating Bralla at 550 mm gives about 7 mm. **7 mm** is chosen: inside the foundry range, at Bralla's value for the longest member. 6 mm, the figure of run 3's comparison, came from general knowledge and is not adopted.
- **Feet 8 mm** (were 10 mm): so that the heaviest section's modulus stays at or below 4 mm, the limit up to which green-sand ductile iron can be cast without risers (QIT/Karsay, below); foot modulus 3.4 mm. The 4:1 taper and ≤ 2:1 section ratio of the design guides hold (8 : 7).
- **Fillets and radii** R10 inside, R4 outside: above the 1/8 in (3 mm) minimum inside radius for ductile iron (Farrar); unchanged.
- **Sections:** depths and flange widths unchanged; the flanges are 1 mm thinner. The seat rail's top (the slat seat) stays where it was; the bolt stack (45 + 7 + 1.6 + 6.8 + 2.5 mm) still takes **M8 x 65**.
- **Casting method:** no risers (directly applied risering), see 4.5. **Corrosion protection:** blast cleaning, hot-dip galvanising (thicker coating on reactive iron), sweep-blast and powder coat, see 4.2.
- **Drawings** at revision C show the material, the 7 mm walls, the 8 mm feet, the coating notes and the gating without risers (008).

## 4. The design and the reasons for it

### 4.1 Ergonomics and overall form

- **Seat height 450 mm** at the front edge, frames 10 mm above the plinth on the anchors' levelling nuts (0–20 mm gives 440–460 mm), grouted after levelling. Inclusive Mobility (2021, p. 102) asks 470–480 mm for most seats; the 2010 ADA Standards (903.5) allow 430–485 mm.
- **Armrests 200 mm above the seat** (Inclusive Mobility); seat width between the armrests 1,540 mm = 3 x 513 mm.
- **Seat tilt 4°, seat-to-back 104°** (back 18° from vertical), within the usual 0–5° and 100–110° (Pheasant and Haslegrave, *Bodyspace*, from general knowledge).
- **Seat depth 400 mm**: 329 mm of slat surface and a 71 mm open throat. **Back:** slats at 71–162 and 268–359 mm above the seat-back junction; top 331 mm above the seat front.

### 4.2 Side frame: ductile cast iron

- One symmetric casting for both sides (VI-C180), T-sections with the flange centred on the web, parting at the web mid-plane, no cores. Members: front post (35 mm, flange 30), armrest (30, pad 60), seat rail (35, flange 68), back rail (30, flange 68), rear leg (35, flange 30); feet 70 x 120 x 8 mm with one Ø14 anchor hole each. Walls 7 mm; pattern shrinkage 1.0 % (metal-castings.com gives 1.0–1.2 % for ductile iron).
- **Material:** EN-GJS-400-15 (EN 1563, 5.3106): Rp0.2 ≥ 250 MPa, Rm ≥ 400 MPa, elongation ≥ 15 % for walls up to 30 mm; density taken as 7100 kg/m³ (EN 1563 summary; foundries quote 7,100–7,300); E about 169 GPa. Heat treatment: as-cast ferritic; ferritising anneal if the thin walls show carbides (thin walls cool fast and can form carbides; general knowledge).
- **Corrosion protection:** EN ISO 1461 applies to castings (≥ 6 mm: at least 80 µm, 575 g/m² mean). Ductile iron holds about 2–3 % silicon, far above the low-reactivity windows of steel (below 0.04 % or 0.15–0.22 % Si, AGA); silicon and phosphorus speed up the growth of the zinc-iron layers, so coatings on cast iron come out thicker and rougher (AGA, 'Galvanizing castings'). The galvanizers' association notes that high-silicon steel reaches about 200 µm nominal. The model therefore takes **about 200 µm**: 0.64 kg per frame (minimum by EN ISO 1461: 0.26 kg). Such thick, mostly alloy-layer coatings are more brittle at edges (general knowledge). ISO 14713-2's own text was not accessible here.
- **Pre-treatment:** chemical cleaning at the galvanizer does not remove moulding sand and surface graphite; the castings are grit- or shot-blasted first (AGA). After galvanising, a sweep-blast or a suitable pre-treatment before the duplex polyester powder coat (70 µm, 0.05 kg) (general knowledge for duplex systems).

### 4.3 Fixings

- Stainless A2-70 carriage bolts **M8 x 65** (ISO 8677), A2-70 nuts, A2 washers. Thermally modified timber is acidic (pH about 4) and the producers' handbook requires stainless A2 or acid-resistant A4 fasteners. **A4 is needed** where chlorides reach the bench, within roughly 1 km of the sea or on salted roads (EN 1993-1-4 Annex A approach, from general knowledge): in the world, on Lisavu's coast and in harbour towns.
- Stainless bolts on a galvanised frame form a bimetallic contact; acceptable in rural and urban air, with isolating washers at the coast (general knowledge).
- Holes pre-drilled (Ø9 in the slats, Ø10 in the flanges); end distance 80 mm = 10 d, edge distance 47.5 mm = 5.9 d (EN 1995-1-1 minima max(7d; 80 mm) and 3d, from general knowledge); square neck seat pre-formed (8.2 mm, 5 mm deep); torque 10 N m. Masses: bolt 30.0 g, washer 1.8 g, nut 5.2 g; 20 sets 0.74 kg, with the bag 0.76 kg.

### 4.5 Casting process (ductile iron)

- **Melting:** blast-furnace hot metal from Serravona ore, desulphurised, adjusted in an induction furnace with returns and steel scrap. Ductile iron needs a low-sulphur base iron; blast-furnace hot metal is a usual base after desulphurisation (general knowledge).
- **Treatment:** magnesium treatment with FeSiMg (about 5-6 % Mg) in a tundish-cover ladle, about 1.3 % addition, residual Mg 0.035-0.05 %; stream inoculation with FeSi75 about 0.2 % (general knowledge; typical practice, not from a source checked here).
- **Mould:** green sand, two frames per mould, parting at the web mid-plane. **Pouring temperature 1400–1440 °C**, held within ±14 °C, as directly applied risering requires (QIT/Karsay notes: pouring temperature for DAR depends on the modulus; thin sections need it high).
- **Risering:** graphite expansion during solidification compensates the shrinkage, so ductile iron needs much smaller risers than steel. The QIT/Karsay rules: in a weak (green sand) mould, a casting whose significant modulus is at most 4 mm is made by **directly applied risering**, the gating or sprue feeding only the liquid contraction; above 4 mm, pressure-control risers; riserless only in rigid moulds above 25 mm. Here the significant modulus is 3.5 mm (bars 3.5 mm, feet 3.4 mm), so **no risers**. Gates of 4 MN x 4 MN = 10 x 10 mm with the contact modulus MN = 2.5 mm (0.7 MS, read from the QIT chart's trend; an assumption), at least 5 times as long as thick (49 mm). Sprue Ø19 mm, runner 26 x 22 mm, pour 23 kg in about 7 s.
- **Result:** castings 20.4 kg per mould, gating and basin 3.6 kg: **poured 24.0 kg, yield 85 %**. Junctions that are thicker than the bars (the main junction J) rely on graphite expansion in a mould that holds; a foundry would confirm with a trial casting or simulation.

## 8. Inconsistencies found

**Resolved by the iron frame (run 4):** run 3's item 19, 'cast steel is unusual for bench ends' (ductile iron is the usual material), and the casting part of the frame-mass item (10.4 kg cast against the world's 10.0). Earlier: the slats' margin (run 3), ash and beech outdoors (run 1), the centre support.

**1. Length 1,800 mm 'over the armrests' with slats of 1,800 mm.** The bolts sit 80 mm from the slat ends; with the frame centre 100 mm in, the 60 mm armrest pads end 70 mm in: **1,660 mm** over the armrests.
*Options:* (a) 1,800 mm over the slats, 1,660 over the armrests; (b) wider pads; (c) longer slats. *Recommendation:* (a).

**2. Side frame 10.0 kg + 0.2 kg zinc (now close for the casting).** In ductile iron at 7 mm the casting weighs 10.41 kg, close to the world's 10.0 kg; but the zinc on reactive iron is about 0.64 kg, not 0.2, and the powder coat adds 0.05 kg: finished 11.09 kg, not 10.2.
*Options:* (a) 10.4 kg cast, 11.1 kg finished; (b) keep 10.0 as the casting and add zinc and coat. *Recommendation:* (a).

**3. Net 43.5 kg leaves out the zinc, and the parcels have weightless cartons.** D20: 2 x 10.0 + 5 x 4.5 + 1.0 = 43.5 kg without zinc; the 10.2 kg frame carton is frame plus zinc. A carton weighs 1.1 kg.
*Options:* (a) net 44.1, gross 46.9 kg. *Recommendation:* (a).

**4. Frame carton 820 x 620 x 80 mm.** The frame is 730 x 620 x 70 mm; the carton 786 x 676 x 98 mm.
*Options:* (a) about 790 x 680 x 100 mm. *Recommendation:* (a).

**5. Pallet of four 1,850 x 650 x 750 mm.** A 676 mm carton does not fit a 650 mm pallet; on 700 mm the load is 744 mm high. Vehicle counts hold.
*Options:* (a) 1,850 x 700 x about 745 mm. *Recommendation:* (a).

*Options:* (a) adopt; (b) 'about 200 kg' (now 8 % off). *Recommendation:* (a).

## 12. Limits of the model

- The design values of the thermally modified ash are an assumption. The DAR contact modulus (0.7 MS) is read from the trend of the QIT chart, not its values; the junctions rely on graphite expansion in green sand. Magnesium treatment, inoculation, pouring speed, melt loss and fettling are typical values (general knowledge).
- The zinc thickness on ductile iron (about 200 µm) is an estimate from the galvanizers' statements about reactive (high-silicon) material; it depends on the iron's silicon and the galvanizer's practice. ISO 14713-2 itself was not accessible.
- Frame: plane-frame model, in-plane bending of T-sections; draft as a mass allowance.
- Read in previews or summaries only: EN 581-2, EN 1563, EN 338, EN 350, EN 1912, EN 1706, ISO 11228-1. From general knowledge: EN 1995-1-1, EN 1990, EN 1993-1-4, EN 1176-1, D40's E0,mean, fastener dimensions, FEFCO 0320, ISPM 15, ISO 668, *Bodyspace*, the reductant rate.
