Kelpstone Gas

#42 Gas Producer · Bayvarra (XB) · Commerce

Produces natural gas from an offshore field off Bayvarra's north coast; treats it at its plant on the shore and sends it by pipeline to the power station and the grid of Urvania. The propane it separates from the field's gas at the plant is sold by road tanker to customers in Bayvarra, among them the peak boilers of Lumeport's district heating (D48).

How UBL helps

Gas is sold by energy, measured at the border, and crosses it by pipeline every day. UBL carries the monthly sale as an Invoice in energy units, the export and import by fixed installation (transport mode 7) in the customs declarations, and a Reminder when the buyer pays late.

In this world it sends 6 and receives 3 documents, and is named in 2 documents between other parties. They are listed per flow below.

Gas and electricity in an unbundled market (Bayvarra's gas field → Urvania)

Urvania's energy market is unbundled, as the EU's is: a power station produces electricity, a grid operator runs the networks and reads the meters, and a retail company buys the energy and bills the customers. The gas comes from an offshore field off Bayvarra's north coast, by pipeline across the border. The documents show the grid operator's metering with the correction a gas meter needs, the wholesale and network invoices, the customs declarations of a pipeline, and a reminder across currencies when the power station pays late.

Propane by road tanker to Lumeport's district heating (dangerous goods, domestic, Bayvarra)

Kelpstone Gas separates propane from its field's gas at the Gullhaven plant and sells it by road tanker. In winter the Coastal State Water Company, which runs Lumeport's district heating, burns propane in its peak boilers and orders a tanker load when its storage runs low. Kelpstone has it carried by Gullhaven Tank Haulage, licensed to carry dangerous goods in tanks: about 20 t of propane, UN 1965, class 2.1, in a tank semi-trailer that weighs about 17 t with its tractor, so within the Accord's 40 t; the tank shows the class 2.1 placard and orange plates with 23 over 1965. The tanker is weighed on the plant's weighbridge empty and full, drives the day to Lumeport, pumps the propane into the water company's storage and returns empty the next day. The propane is burned, not resold, so Bayvarra's sales tax applies to the sale (D48, O204).
