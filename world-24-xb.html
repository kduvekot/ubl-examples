How UBL helps

A factor buys the distributor's invoices and collects the payment. UBL invoices can name the factor as payee, with its account, so the buyer pays the right party without a separate letter.

In this world it sends 1 and receives 1 documents, and is named in 2 documents between other parties. They are listed per flow below.

Distributor to retailer, delivered duty paid (domestic, Bayvarra)

The retailer buys delivered duty paid (DDP) at its shop: the distributor arranges the transport and bears all costs, so the distributor, not the retailer, books the truck and receives the transport documents. The retailer shares forecasts, promotions and its stock levels so the distributor can plan replenishment. The store renovates the home and garden department of its Lumeport store and reopens it on Monday 11 May, with two weeks at 10 % off; to set up the range it asks the distributor for a catalogue, which the distributor deletes when its winter catalogue takes over on 20 June. The store orders 24 benches, delivered in a slot on the morning of 30 April; it refuses one in a torn carton, and the distributor credits it. The distributor has sold its receivables to a factor, which the store pays. A consumer buys in the shop, and another orders a dining set from the store's web shop.

Overseas by container ship, cost insurance and freight (Bayvarra → island Lisavu)

The distributor wins a new customer on the island. It introduces itself, both join the Trade Accord's digital agreement, and the buyer orders 104 benches and spare frames, cost, insurance and freight (CIF) to the port of discharge: 96 benches for the Port of Selanu's harbour parks and two more pallets of four for its own stock. The prices, 520 lume a bench and 68 a frame, hold the sea freight and the cargo insurance, which the distributor buys through its freight forwarder; the invoice bills the goods alone. The forwarder clears the export and books the container; the carrier reports its ship and cargo to the island's authorities before it arrives; the buyer's customs broker clears the goods and its 3PL receives them. The distributor has sold the receivable to a factor, which sends the statement of account. A frame arrives crushed and a pallet's illegible mark makes customs inspect the container, so the distributor credits the frame and the cost of the inspection, and the buyer pays the factor. The buyer sells two of the benches in its shop.
