How UBL helps

A government information service publishes procurement notices and keeps the business registry. UBL notices reach it in one structured format, and business information documents let it share registry data that tenderers can reuse as evidence.

In this world it sends 4 and receives 9 documents, and is named in 0 documents between other parties. They are listed per flow below.

A public tender (Bayvarra)

The contracting authority buys park benches for the city's parks under procurement law, in two lots: the benches and their installation. The notices are published through the government information service; the manufacturer tenders alone, with an engineering firm as its subcontractor for the installation, backed by a bank guarantee; a furniture maker tenders wooden benches, backed by pledged government bonds. After an electronic auction and the award the authority orders the first release under the framework contract; the manufacturer delivers and invoices the benches, the subcontractor installs them, the authority accepts the release, and the installation is invoiced once accepted.

A design contest for the promenade bench (Bayvarra)

The contracting authority relaunches a design contest for the bench of Lumeport's harbour promenade, after cancelling the first one because no design met the brief. The council raises the prize fund before the contest notice; a corrigendum corrects the site plan, extends the deadline for requests and lets an industrial designer lead a design. The furniture maker and the engineering firm enter as a team, and the furniture maker's own designer takes over the lead; every member of the team is checked in the register, and a small design studio is excluded at the selection. The jury awards the first prize to the team and the second to the manufacturer; the results notice is corrected for a prize amount. Several documents are revised and say which version they supersede. No goods move.
