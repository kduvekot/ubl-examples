#8 Seller's Warehouse (3PL)

Country A (XA) · Commerce

Picks, packs and hands the goods to the carrier for the manufacturer; knows nothing about prices.

How UBL helps

A third-party warehouse acts on instructions from its client but never sees the commercial terms. UBL separates the two: the warehouse sends despatch advices and inventory reports with exactly the logistic details (packages, weights, serial numbers) while prices stay in the documents between seller and buyer.

In this world it sends 1 and receives 1 documents, and is named in 1 documents between other parties. They are listed per flow below.

Finished goods by truck across the land border (Country A → Country B)

The distributor buys free carrier (FCA) at the manufacturer's warehouse. Because the buyer arranges the transport, the distributor books the road carrier itself and tells the seller in the order which truck company will pick the goods up and where. The seller's 3PL hands the goods over; the seller clears them for export, the distributor for import.

Sends

  • Despatch Advice to #6 Distributor: The 3PL picks and loads the goods for the manufacturer and announces the despatch; it knows the goods and the truck but not the prices.

    In it as DespatchSupplierParty.

    Also mentions: #5 Manufacturer as SellerSupplierParty; #13 Road Carrier as Shipment/Consignment/CarrierParty.

Receives

  • Receipt Advice from #6 Distributor: The distributor confirms what arrived to the party that despatched it, the 3PL, naming the manufacturer as seller.

    In it as DespatchSupplierParty.

    Also mentions: #5 Manufacturer as SellerSupplierParty.

Is named in documents between others

  • Order from #6 Distributor to #5 Manufacturer: Delivery terms FCA seller's warehouse: the order names the warehouse where the goods are collected and the road carrier the distributor has booked to collect them.

    In it as Delivery/Despatch/DespatchParty (The Party who despatches the goods).

    Also mentions: #13 Road Carrier as Delivery/Despatch/CarrierParty.
